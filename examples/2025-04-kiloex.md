# KiloEx — permissionless forwarder `execute` let anyone open and close positions at manipulated prices — KiloEx perpetual DEX / BNB Chain, Base, Taiko — 2025-04-14

**Loss:** **~\$7.5M** per [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/kiloex-exploiter-returns-5-5-m-7-5-m-dex-exploit) and [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/kiloex-recovers-7-5m-after-promising-attacker-10-bounty/); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/351839/kiloex-price-exploit-compensation) and [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2025/04/18/kiloexs-sophisticated-hack-shows-defi-risks-but-this-time-recovery-was-swift) say about \$7M. Chains differ by source: Base, Taiko and BNB Chain per [Cyvers](https://x.com/CyversAlerts/status/1912868664946335888) and The Block; Base, opBNB and BSC per PeckShield as relayed by [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/kiloex-reveals-7m-smart-contract-exploit-in-post-mortem-report). Recovery: on 2025-04-18 [KiloEx stated](https://x.com/KiloEx_perp/status/1913168299292328115) it had recovered all stolen funds and would pay 10% of the recovered amount as a bounty. The same day PeckShield posted about \$5.5M returned, as relayed by [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/kiloex-exploiter-returns-5-5-m-7-5-m-dex-exploit); the article does not reconcile the two figures.

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.004** (Access-Control Misconfiguration — *primary*, per the post-mortem as relayed by [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/kiloex-reveals-7m-smart-contract-exploit-in-post-mortem-report)). The flaw was in the TrustedForwarder contract, which inherited OpenZeppelin's MinimalForwarderUpgradeable and did not override its `execute` method, so the method stayed permissionless; the attacker used it to open and close positions at favourable prices. See [`techniques/T9.004-access-control-misconfiguration.md`](../techniques/T9.004-access-control-misconfiguration.md). Public sources call the result an oracle or price problem ([Cyvers](https://x.com/CyversAlerts/status/1912868664946335888): "a suspected access control flaw in the price oracle"); OAK did not open the post-mortem itself, so no second Technique is claimed.

**Attribution:** **unattributed.** No source opened names the attacker. Cyvers and [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/kiloex-recovers-7-5m-after-promising-attacker-10-bounty/) say the exploiting wallet was funded through Tornado Cash. Attacker addresses are not printed in full on any opened page.

**Key teaching point:** **A forwarder or relayer that executes calls on behalf of users must be restricted at the contract level to the callers (or signers) the protocol intends; inheriting a library's `execute` unchanged leaves it open to everyone.** Per the post-mortem as relayed, that one unchanged method let anyone act on the protocol across several chains within an hour.

## Summary

KiloEx is a perpetual-futures exchange deployed on several chains. Per [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/kiloex-reveals-7m-smart-contract-exploit-in-post-mortem-report), citing the 2025-04-21 post-mortem, its TrustedForwarder inherited MinimalForwarderUpgradeable and did not override `execute`, which left it permissionless. The attacker abused it to open and close positions at favourable prices on several chains, and the attack took under an hour. [Cyvers](https://x.com/CyversAlerts/status/1912868664946335888) says it flagged the activity before the attack was publicly confirmed. [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/351839/kiloex-price-exploit-compensation) reports that KiloEx paused trading and, the day after, asked the attacker to return most of the funds and keep 10%. On 2025-04-18 [KiloEx announced](https://x.com/KiloEx_perp/status/1913168299292328115) recovery of all stolen funds.
