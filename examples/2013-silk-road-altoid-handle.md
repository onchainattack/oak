# Silk Road "altoid" handle cross-layer de-anonymization — 2013

**Loss:** **Attribution event, not financial loss.** The "altoid" username cross-layer de-anonymization was the operational-security failure that enabled the FBI to identify Ross Ulbricht as Dread Pirate Roberts, the operator of Silk Road — the largest darknet marketplace of its era (~\$1.2B in cumulative sales at later bitcoin prices, 2011–2013, per `[wikisralt]`).
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T8.005** (Operational Security Procedural Failure) — canonical anchor. "altoid" promoted Silk Road on shroomery.org and bitcointalk.org in January 2011, and on 2011-10-11 posted on bitcointalk.org seeking an IT professional with replies directed to `rossulbricht at gmail dot com`, linking the operator persona to Ulbricht's real name by handle and email reuse (`[nbcaltoid]`, `[schneieraltoid]`).
**Attribution:** **confirmed** — Ross Ulbricht was convicted on 2015-02-04 and sentenced on 2015-05-29; he received a full pardon on 2025-01-21 (`[wikiulbrichtaltoid]`). The "altoid" linkage was cited in the FBI's criminal complaint (`[nbcaltoid]`).

**Corrected 2026-10-11:** The Gmail address appeared in an October 2011 Bitcoin Talk post (not a shroomery post); a March 2012 Stack Overflow account is added; sentence and 2025 pardon added; the "laptop with DPR admin session active" detail is removed as unsourced here.
**Key teaching point:** The Silk Road "altoid" failure is the textbook T8.005 anchor: a single pseudonym reused across the clearweb and darknet surfaces linked the operator's anonymous marketplace persona to a verified real-world identity. The case established the operational template for law-enforcement attribution of darknet marketplace operators — cross-layer pseudonym correlation via OSINT, followed by physical-surveillance confirmation.

## Summary

Ross Ulbricht operated Silk Road under the pseudonym "Dread Pirate Roberts" (DPR) from 2011 to 2013. Silk Road was a Tor-hidden-service marketplace facilitating approximately \$1.2 billion in cumulative sales at later bitcoin prices (about \$183 million at the time of the transactions), primarily for narcotics, using Bitcoin as the payment rail (`[wikisralt]`).

In January 2011 "altoid" promoted Silk Road on shroomery.org and, a few days later, on bitcointalk.org (`[nbcaltoid]`). On 2011-10-11 "altoid" posted on bitcointalk.org for an "IT pro in the Bitcoin community" and gave `rossulbricht at gmail dot com` as the contact (`[nbcaltoid]`, `[schneieraltoid]`). IRS-CI agent Gary Alford found the "altoid" posts while searching for early mentions of the site (`[wikiulbrichtaltoid]`). A Stack Overflow account under the name Ross Ulbricht with the same email was created in early March 2012 (`[schneieraltoid]`).

The "altoid" linkage was cited in the criminal complaint. Ulbricht was arrested on 2013-10-01 at the Glen Park branch of the San Francisco Public Library (`[wikiulbrichtaltoid]`). The FBI said the server was in Reykjavik, Iceland, though security experts doubted its account of how it found it (`[wikisralt]`). Ulbricht was convicted on 2015-02-04 on all counts and sentenced on 2015-05-29 (`[wikiulbrichtaltoid]`).
