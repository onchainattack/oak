# Balancer V2 — rounding in the stable-pool upscale step let one batchSwap suppress the pool-token price and drain ComposableStablePools — Balancer V2 / Ethereum and other EVM chains — 2025-11-03

**Loss:** **\$116M to \$128.64M, depending on the source.** [Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/) reports \$128.64M across six networks in under 30 minutes. [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/balancer-post-mortem-report-116m-hack), relaying Balancer's preliminary post-mortem of 2025-11-05, reports about \$116M. [OpenZeppelin](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/understanding-the-balancer-v2-exploit) reports more than \$120M across Balancer and its forks, and [BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/in-depth-analysis-the-balancer-v2-exploit) more than \$125M. No opened page gives a per-chain split. Recovery: Cointelegraph, relaying the preliminary post-mortem, reports that Balancer and partners clawed back or froze part of the funds, including 5,041 osETH (about \$19M) and 13,495 osGNO (up to \$2M). Gnosis Chain validators later froze about \$9.4M with a soft fork and moved it with a hard fork activated on 2025-12-22 ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/gnosis-chain-activates-hard-fork-to-recover-9-4m-frozen-during-balancer-exploit/)).

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.011** (Precision-Loss Rounding Attack — *primary*). Check Point locates the precision loss in `_upscaleArray`, which scales balances with `FixedPoint.mulDown`, so the pool invariant D is underestimated and the BPT price (D / totalSupply) is pushed down ([Check Point](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/)). OpenZeppelin traces the same flaw to `_upscale` calling `mulDown` whatever the swap direction, with non-unitary scaling factors from the `_scalingFactors` override ([OpenZeppelin](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/understanding-the-balancer-v2-exploit)). BlockSec describes upscaling that rounds one way against downscaling that rounds both ways ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/in-depth-analysis-the-balancer-v2-exploit)). This is rounding in the protocol's own arithmetic with no oracle and no re-entry, which is what [`techniques/T9.011-precision-loss-rounding-attack.md`](../techniques/T9.011-precision-loss-rounding-attack.md) describes. The earlier mapping to the reentrancy Technique is dropped: none of Check Point, OpenZeppelin or BlockSec describes a re-entrant call. The flash-loan Technique is not tagged either. Cointelegraph, relaying Balancer's preliminary report, says the attacker used flash loans; OpenZeppelin, BlockSec and Check Point do not mention them. The sources disagree and no flash-loan transaction is cited, so OAK does not tag it.

**Attribution:** **unattributed.** [Check Point](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/) names no actor and gives only the addresses below. The earlier file's suggestion of a DPRK association is not in any opened page and is removed.

**Key teaching point:** **Round every scaling step in the direction that favours the pool, and test pool math at near-empty balances before shipping a change to the scaling factors.** OpenZeppelin finds that the audited contracts did not contain the `_scalingFactors` override that returns non-unitary values, so the exploited code was added after its audits ([OpenZeppelin](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/understanding-the-balancer-v2-exploit)). BlockSec reports that the protocol could not be paused ([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/in-depth-analysis-the-balancer-v2-exploit)).

## Summary

Balancer V2's ComposableStablePool contracts scale token balances before running the stable-swap invariant. On 2025-11-03 an attacker called `batchSwap` on the V2 Vault so that, per OpenZeppelin, the pool balances were driven very low and a repeating prime, exploit and reset sequence of swaps made the settled deltas larger than they should be ([OpenZeppelin](https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/understanding-the-balancer-v2-exploit)). Check Point says the first stage ran from the exploit contract's constructor and left the proceeds as internal balances in the Vault; a second transaction then withdrew them ([Check Point](https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/)). Balancer published a preliminary post-mortem on 2025-11-05 naming a rounding error in the upscale function for exact-out swaps and listing Stable Pools and Composable Stable v5 pools as affected ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/balancer-post-mortem-report-116m-hack)).

## Root-cause evidence

- **Technique:** OAK-T9.011
- **Source:** <https://research.checkpoint.com/2025/how-an-attacker-drained-128m-from-balancer-through-rounding-error-exploitation/>; <https://www.openzeppelin.com/news/understanding-the-balancer-v2-exploit>; <https://blocksec.com/blog/in-depth-analysis-the-balancer-v2-exploit>; <https://cointelegraph.com/news/balancer-post-mortem-report-116m-hack>
- **Source type:** security-firm
- **Confidence:** corroborated
- **Evidence:** Three independent security firms (Check Point, OpenZeppelin, BlockSec) put the root cause in rounding in the upscale step of the stable-pool math combined with `batchSwap`, and Cointelegraph reports that Balancer's own preliminary post-mortem names a rounding error in the exact-out upscale function. Balancer's post-mortem on Medium (<https://medium.com/balancer-protocol/nov-3-exploit-post-mortem-51dcbeb6b020>) could not be opened, so the mechanism is not marked `confirmed`. Open: whether flash loans were used (Cointelegraph says yes, three firms do not mention them), the per-chain loss split, and where the proceeds went.
