# Thala Labs — missing check that an unstake is no larger than the stake, in a v1 farming contract — Thala / Aptos — 2024-11-15

**Loss:** **~\$25.5M** of THALA-LP tokens withdrawn from the v1 farming contracts, per [Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024) (published 2024-11-18). [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught) (2024-11-18) gives the same \$25.5M as the value of the stolen liquidity-pool tokens and adds that about \$2.5M in THL tokens and about \$9M from the Move Dollar (MOD) stablecoin were stolen. **Recovered:** all of it. Thala negotiated the return of user assets for a \$300,000 bounty, and per the on-chain sleuth Ogle the funds came back about six hours after the incident ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught)). Thala also froze \$11.5M in Thala-related assets ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught)); Halborn puts this at about half of the stolen assets ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024)). Thala said affected users need no action and positions "will be made 100% whole" ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught)).

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.016** (Contract Accounting / State-Invariant Flaw, *primary*). Per Halborn, a recent update to one of the v1 farming contracts failed to validate that a withdrawal of staked tokens was no larger than the user's total staked amount; the attacker unstaked far more THALA-LP than they had staked ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024)). The caller was entitled to call the function and the contract's bookkeeping was wrong, which is the T9.016 shape. See [`techniques/T9.016-contract-accounting-state-invariant-flaw.md`](../techniques/T9.016-contract-accounting-state-invariant-flaw.md). The earlier version of this file filed the flaw under T9.004 as a missing authority check on reward claims; no opened source says that.

**Attribution:** **unattributed.** Thala said "With the help of law enforcement, Seal 911, Ogle, and others, we were able to quickly identify the exploiter"; the attacker's identity was not disclosed ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught)). Cointelegraph reports no arrest or charge, and the earlier claim of a Hong Kong police arrest on 2024-11-18 is not supported by any source opened for this rewrite.

**Key teaching point:** **When a farming or staking contract is changed, test the invariant that an unstake never exceeds the staked amount on every withdrawal path of the new code.** The flaw entered in a recent update to a v1 contract that was still live ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024)). Per Halborn, Move's design let Thala freeze about half of the stolen assets quickly ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024)).

## Summary

On 2024-11-15 Thala Labs, a DeFi platform on Aptos, suffered a security breach caused by what it called an "isolated vulnerability" in its v1 farming contracts, which let the attacker withdraw liquidity tokens; Thala announced it on 2024-11-16 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught)). Per Halborn, the attacker added liquidity to a vulnerable pool and received THALA-LP tokens, staked and unstaked them down to a zero staked balance, then unstaked a large amount anyway, and swapped the tokens for lzUSDC ([Halborn](https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024)). Thala paused the relevant contracts and froze \$11.5M in Thala-related assets, then negotiated the return of the assets for a \$300,000 bounty ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught)). Farming stayed paused pending a review and re-audit of the codebase as of that article. Function names, contract addresses and transaction hashes are not on either page opened; they are undisclosed here.

## Root-cause evidence

- **Technique:** OAK-T9.016
- **Source:** <https://www.halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-thala-hack-november-2024>; <https://cointelegraph.com/news/thala-recovers-25-million-exploiter-hacker-caught>
- **Source type:** security-firm
- **Confidence:** reported
- **Evidence:** Halborn, a third-party analysis, describes the flaw as a failure to validate that the unstaked amount is less than the user's total staked amount, introduced in a recent update to a v1 farming contract. Cointelegraph, citing Thala's announcement, confirms a vulnerability in the v1 farming contracts that let the hacker withdraw liquidity tokens. The Thala post-mortem itself was not opened (Halborn and Cointelegraph link to it from X posts), so the exact code path is not independently confirmed.
