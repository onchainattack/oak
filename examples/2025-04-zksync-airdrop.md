# ZKsync — compromised 1/1 admin key minted the unclaimed airdrop tokens from three Merkle distributors — ZKsync Era / Ethereum — 2025-04-13

**Loss:** **111,881,122 ZK** (about \$5M at the time of the first transaction), per the [ZKsync incident report](https://www.zksync.io/blog/incident-report-compromised-admin-key). The attacker swapped about 67,193,843 ZK for ETH and withdrew about 1,116 ETH to Ethereum mainnet (same report). **Returned:** on 2025-04-23 the attacker returned 90% under a safe-harbor offer, in three transfers worth almost \$5.7M at the time, per [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/zk-sync-recovers-5-million-stolen-tokens-hacker-accepts-bounty); the figure exceeds the original because ZK and ETH rose after the attack (same article). The returned funds are in the ZKsync Security Council's custody, and governance has not decided their use (incident report). ZKsync says user funds, the protocol, the ZK token contract and the governance contracts were not affected.

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.014** (Operator Privileged-Key Compromise — *primary*, operator-stated). The admin of three ZK Merkle distributors was a 1/1 Safe multisig that was never moved under the Token Governor or given the 3/5 threshold used for other contracts; its key was compromised and used to call `sweepUnclaimed()`, minting the remaining unclaimed supply ([incident report](https://www.zksync.io/blog/incident-report-compromised-admin-key)). How the key was obtained is unknown, so no entry-vector Technique is claimed. See [`techniques/T11.014-operator-privileged-key-compromise.md`](../techniques/T11.014-operator-privileged-key-compromise.md).

**Attribution:** **unattributed.** The report says the hacker's identity is unknown. It also says the multisig was generated by a former ZKsync contributor and that the investigation found no evidence of malicious intent by that person. The hacker address is [`0xb1027ED67f89c9F588E097f70807163feC1005d3`](https://explorer.zksync.io/address/0xb1027ED67f89c9F588E097f70807163feC1005d3).

**Key teaching point:** **Every privileged key that can mint or sweep tokens must sit behind the same multi-party threshold and governance path as the rest of the protocol, and a contract whose admin has not changed since deployment must be re-reviewed after its risk changes.** Per the report, the root cause was a procedural error that misclassified the distributors' risk, so their security configuration was never updated.

## Summary

For the June 2024 ZK airdrop, three Merkle distributor contracts held the unclaimed supply. Their admin was a 1/1 Safe multisig, unchanged since deployment. After the distribution period ended on 2025-01-03, `sweepUnclaimed()` became callable. On 2025-04-13 the attacker used the compromised admin to mint the remaining unclaimed tokens, then swapped ZK for ETH until Matter Labs filtered transactions on 2025-04-15. [ZKsync disclosed the incident on 2025-04-15](https://www.theblock.co/post/350839/zksync-discloses-5-million-attack-compromised-airdrop-contract-admin-account) and said user funds were never at risk. The Security Council posted an on-chain safe-harbor message on 2025-04-21; the attacker returned 90% on 2025-04-23. Press reports date the attack to 2025-04-15 (the day of disclosure); the incident report gives 2025-04-13.

## Root-cause evidence

- **Technique:** OAK-T11.014
- **Source:** <https://www.zksync.io/blog/incident-report-compromised-admin-key>
- **Source type:** operator
- **Confidence:** confirmed
- **Evidence:** The operator's incident report states that a compromised admin account minted the unclaimed tokens through `sweepUnclaimed()`, that the admin was a 1/1 Safe never assigned to the Token Governor, and that the root cause was a procedural error in classifying the distributors' risk. Unknown per the report: the hacker's identity and how the signer key was accessed.
