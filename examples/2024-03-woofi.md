# WOOFi Swap sPMM pricing error priced WOO near zero under flash loans — WOOFi Swap / Arbitrum — 2024-03-05

**Loss:** WOOFi's own post-mortem puts the attacker's net profit at about \$8.75M after the flash loans were repaid ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem), repeated by [Halborn](https://halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-woofi-hack-march-2024)). Rekt News gives about \$8.5M ([Rekt](https://rekt.news/woo-rekt)). The two figures are not reconciled in the sources. Only WOOFi Swap on Arbitrum was affected; WOOFi Stake, Earn and Pro were not ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). WOOFi offered the exploiter a 10% bounty and placed a separate Arkham bounty ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). Recovery of funds is not reported in the sources opened.

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.001** (Oracle Price Manipulation — *primary*). The sPMM pricing logic is built to override the oracle price according to the notional value of user trades; an error pushed the WOO price far outside the expected range, to \$0.00000009, and the fallback check against Chainlink did not cover the WOO price ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). See [`techniques/T9.001-oracle-price-manipulation.md`](../techniques/T9.001-oracle-price-manipulation.md). **OAK-T9.002** (Flash-Loan-Enabled Exploit — *enabling*). The attacker borrowed about 7.7M WOO in flash loans and sold it into WOOFi Swap ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). See [`techniques/T9.002-flash-loan-enabled-exploit.md`](../techniques/T9.002-flash-loan-enabled-exploit.md).

**Attribution:** **unattributed**. The sources name no person or group. WOOFi sent an on-chain message to the attacker address "assuming a whitehat" ([Rekt](https://rekt.news/woo-rekt)).

**Key teaching point:** **A price-adjustment path that can override the oracle needs its own hard bound and its own fallback check for every token it prices.** WOOFi's Chainlink fallback did not include WOO, so the adjusted price of \$0.00000009 was accepted ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). The operator ties the exploitability to a recently added WOO lending market on Arbitrum, which made flash-loan capital available, and to low WOO liquidity elsewhere on the network ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). Adding a lending market for a token is a change to the threat model of every pricing contract that touches that token.

## Summary

WOOFi Swap prices trades with its sPMM algorithm, which overrides the oracle price based on the notional value of user trades to adjust slippage and balance pools ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). At 15:49 UTC on 2024-03-05 the algorithm was exploited on Arbitrum: a previously unidentified error adjusted the WOO price far outside the expected range ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). The attacker borrowed about 7.7M WOO plus other assets through a series of flash loans, sold the WOO into WOOFi, and then took about 10M WOO out at almost no cost; this was repeated three times, which Halborn reports as within 13 minutes ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem), [Halborn](https://halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-woofi-hack-march-2024)). Rekt News describes the pool drained via the swap function three times ([Rekt](https://rekt.news/woo-rekt)).

WOOFi says the fallback check, normally against Chainlink, did not cover the WOO token price, and that the attack was feasible because of a recent WOO lending market on Arbitrum and low WOO liquidity elsewhere on the network ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). WOOFi Swap is deployed on more than 10 networks; per the operator no other network had both the WOO token and a WOO lending market, so the exploit could not be replicated there ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)). The contracts were paused by 16:02 UTC ([WOOFi post-mortem](https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem)).

## Root-cause evidence

- **Technique:** OAK-T9.001
- **Source:** <https://woox.io/blog/woofi-spmm-exploit-post-mortem>; <https://halborn.com/blog/post/explained-the-woofi-hack-march-2024>; <https://rekt.news/woo-rekt>
- **Source type:** operator
- **Confidence:** corroborated
- **Evidence:** The operator post-mortem states the mechanism (sPMM price adjusted outside the expected range, Chainlink fallback not covering WOO, flash-loaned WOO sold in). Halborn repeats it and Rekt describes the same flash-loan price manipulation. These pages restate the operator account rather than adding independent forensics. The nature of the "previously unidentified error" in the sPMM code is not described in any source opened. The loss figure differs between sources (\$8.75M net profit vs \$8.5M).
