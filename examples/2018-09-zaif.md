# Zaif exchange hack — Multi-asset (BTC, BCH, MONA) — 2018-09-14

**Loss:** approximately 6.7 billion yen (~\$60M) in Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash and MonaCoin from Japanese exchange Zaif (5,966 BTC per Asia Times). Of the total, about 2.2 billion yen belonged to Tech Bureau and 4.5 billion yen to customers (SiliconANGLE-listed reporting). Hot-wallet compromise.
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.001** (Third-Party Signing-Vendor UI / Signing-Flow Compromise — broadly construed; the exchange's hot-wallet signing infrastructure was compromised directly, enabling the multi-asset extraction). **OAK-T15.003** (Operator-Endpoint Compromise — reported by an FSA official to Asia Times (secondhand), Tech Bureau told the regulator that "an employee's PC was hacked"; the FSA's earlier orders documented control deficiencies).
**Attribution:** **unattributed** (was "confirmed" until 2026-10-11, which contradicted the sentence that follows). Japanese authorities investigated but did not publicly attribute the breach to a specific threat actor or nation-state group.
**Corrected 2026-10-11:** removed the unsourced "4-day disclosure delay / third order" timeline and the claim that Fisco committed to full user reimbursement and became a Bitpoint/DMM template; the sourced facts are a two-hour theft on 2018-09-14, discovery on 09-17, prior FSA orders in March and June 2018, a post-hack improvement order, and Fisco's majority-stake rescue. The attribution word above is inconsistent with the text (no public attribution) and should be reviewed.

**Key teaching point:** **Zaif is the canonical Japanese-exchange regulatory-failure case: the attacker reached the exchange's hot wallets, the Japan FSA had already issued business-improvement orders to Zaif's operator Tech Bureau in March and June 2018, and the FSA said after the breach that the company had not given adequate details or explained the reporting delay.** Fisco later took majority control of Zaif in exchange for financial support (reported).

## Summary

Zaif, a Japanese cryptocurrency exchange operated by Osaka-based Tech Bureau Corp, was hacked on 2018-09-14 over a roughly two-hour period (Japanese media via Asia Times); the theft was not detected until 2018-09-17. About 7 billion yen (~\$60M) was taken, including 5,966 BTC plus Bitcoin Cash and MonaCoin. Attackers reportedly accessed the exchange's hot wallets, and an FSA official said Tech Bureau told the regulator that an employee's PC was hacked.

The FSA had issued business-improvement orders to Tech Bureau in March 2018 (risk-management system and customer response) and June 2018 (business management, legal compliance, AML/CFT risk management, segregation of user property). After the hack it issued a further business-improvement order asking how the company would compensate customers, said Tech Bureau had not provided adequate details nor explained the reporting delay, and warned of further action.

Zaif then agreed a rescue in which Fisco Digital Asset Group provided financial support in exchange for majority control of the company (reported; full terms not verified here).

## Realised extraction

About 6.7-7 billion yen (~\$60M) in BTC, BCH and MONA. No recovery is established in the sources cited. Customer compensation arrangements were announced but their completion is not verified here.

## Regulatory context

The Zaif breach is a case of a known-deficient operator being hit after regulatory warnings but before remediation was shown to be complete: two FSA orders preceded the hack.
