# Bitcoin Savings & Trust Ponzi — Bitcoin — 2011-02 to 2012-08

**Loss:** more than 700,000 bitcoins taken from investors, February 2011 through August 2012, per the SEC `[btcstsec]`. Trendon Shavers, operating under the online pseudonym "Pirateat40," solicited Bitcoin-denominated investments through Bitcoin Savings & Trust (BTCST) online, including on the Bitcoin Forum, promising returns of up to 7% weekly paid in bitcoins `[btcstsec]`. The earlier text's "~100 investors" and dollar-equivalent figures were unsourced and removed.
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.005.002** (Fake-Custodian / Fake-Asset-Manager Fraud) — primary; Shavers presented BTCS&T as a legitimate Bitcoin-denominated investment vehicle with Bitcoin arbitrage trading as the yield source, but the operation was a Ponzi scheme where existing investors were paid with new investors' deposits. **OAK-T3.001** (Pump-and-Dump / Coordinated Social-Media Promotion) — the investment solicitation was propagated through the Bitcoin Talk forum (the dominant social platform for the 2011-2012 Bitcoin community), making this the earliest documented case of social-platform-distributed crypto investment fraud. **OAK-T5.005** (Treasury-Management Exit — broadly construed at the Ponzi level; the operator accumulated investor deposits and progressively diverted them for personal use rather than conducting the claimed trading activity).
**Attribution:** **confirmed**. The SEC civil action against Shavers and BTCST is SEC v. Shavers, Civil Action No. 4:13-CV-416 (U.S. District Court, Sherman, Texas). On 2014-09-18 the court entered final judgment: \$39,638,569 disgorgement plus \$1,766,098 prejudgment interest (\$40,404,667 total, joint and several) and a \$150,000 civil penalty each for Shavers and BTCST; the court found BTCST "a sham and a Ponzi scheme" `[btcstsec]`. Shavers was arrested in November 2014 on securities and wire fraud charges and later pleaded guilty to one count of securities fraud `[btcstdoj]``[btcstie]`. The sentence is not established by the cited sources (earlier text said 18 months).
**Key teaching point:** **Bitcoin Savings & Trust is the canonical 2011-2012 worked example of the three structural primitives that became the dominant crypto fraud template: (1) social-platform-distributed investment solicitation (Bitcoin Talk forum → modern X/Twitter, Discord, Telegram), (2) fake-asset-manager fraud with a fictitious yield-generation narrative (Bitcoin arbitrage → modern DeFi yield, MEV, AI trading), and (3) Ponzi payout structure masking extraction (early investors paid with later investors' deposits).** The case is the earliest SEC enforcement action classifying Bitcoin-denominated investments as securities, establishing the legal framework that underpins modern crypto-enforcement.
**Corrected 2026-10-11:** Scheme dates now follow the SEC (Feb 2011 to Aug 2012, more than 700,000 BTC); the 2014 judgment was a final judgment totalling \$40,404,667 plus \$150,000 penalties; removed the unsourced '~100 investors', wire-fraud conviction and 18-month sentence.

## Summary

Trendon Shavers solicited investments in "Bitcoin Savings & Trust" under the pseudonym "Pirateat40," including on the Bitcoin Forum, claiming a Bitcoin arbitrage operation and promising up to 7% weekly returns paid in bitcoins `[btcstsec]`.

Between February 2011 and August 2012 more than 700,000 bitcoins were taken from investors `[btcstsec]`. The SEC found the operation a sham and a Ponzi scheme `[btcstsec]`. Claims here that Shavers day-traded and diverted funds for personal expenses, and that BTCST was the largest Bitcoin-denominated vehicle of its era, are not supported by the cited sources.

The scheme ran through August 2012 `[btcstsec]`. The SEC filed its civil action in July 2013; a district court ruling in August 2013 reportedly held that BTCST investments were securities (not re-verified in this pass). The final judgment of 2014-09-18 is above; earlier text calling it a default judgment, and the 18-month sentence, are unverified.

The case's structural signature — social-platform-distributed fake-investment solicitation with Ponzi payout mechanics — is the direct ancestor of the 2017-2019 ICO-era Ponzi schemes, the 2019 PlusToken \$2B+ scheme (T11.005.002 at scale), the 2020-2022 DeFi yield-farm Ponzi cohort, and the 2023-2026 pig-butchering / fake-CEX fraud class (T11.005.001). The forum-distribution primitive (Bitcoin Talk 2011) is the structural template for Telegram/Discord/X-distributed investment fraud in the modern era.
