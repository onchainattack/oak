# CoinEx exchange hot-wallet compromise — Multi-chain (ETH, TRON, Polygon) — 2023-09-12

**Loss:** approximately \$53-54M per address-tracking firms (SecurityWeek: roughly \$53M; Risky Business: about \$54M) across Ether, Tron and Matic (Polygon) assets and other chains `[riskybizcoinex]` `[securityweekcoinex]`. CoinEx itself did not disclose the amount `[securityweekcoinex]`. CoinEx's preliminary cause was the leakage of hot-wallet private keys `[securityweekcoinex]`.

**Corrected 2026-10-11:** loss restated from \$54-70M to ~\$53-54M (the \$70M figure appears in one low-quality source only); cause stated as hot-wallet private-key leakage per CoinEx; the claimed FBI/CISA joint advisory attributing CoinEx is removed — cited sources show suspicion by Elliptic/CertiK/ZachXBT via links to Stake.com laundering addresses, and the FBI attributing Stake.com, not CoinEx; the reimbursement programme and post-mortem claims are unverified.
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.001** (Third-Party Signing-Vendor UI / Signing-Flow Compromise — broadly construed; hot-wallet private keys leaked, and hot wallets were drained across multiple chains `[securityweekcoinex]`). **OAK-T7.001** (Mixer-Routed Laundering — some stolen funds went to an address that also held funds from the Stake.com hack `[riskybizcoinex]`).
**OAK-Gnn:** [OAK-G01 Lazarus Group / DPRK-attributed](../actors/OAK-G01-lazarus.md).
**Attribution:** **inferred-strong** CoinEx did not formally link the hack to North Korea. ZachXBT found that some stolen CoinEx funds went to the same address holding funds from the Stake.com hack, and a CertiK report confirmed that and noted further links `[riskybizcoinex]`; SecurityWeek reports CertiK says Lazarus is responsible and links it to the Atomic Wallet, Alphapo, CoinsPaid and Stake.com heists `[securityweekcoinex]`. No FBI or CISA statement specific to CoinEx is cited here (the FBI linked Stake.com, not CoinEx, to Lazarus `[riskybizcoinex]`). The `inferred-strong` rating therefore rests on private-firm on-chain linkage.
**Key teaching point:** **CoinEx shows that leaked hot-wallet private keys can drain an exchange across several independent chains at once (Ether, Tron, Matic) `[riskybizcoinex]`; one key-custody failure is a multi-chain failure.** Attribution to Lazarus rests on on-chain links to Stake.com proceeds, not on a government finding.

## Summary

CoinEx, a Hong Kong-based cryptocurrency exchange founded in 2017, suffered a coordinated hot-wallet compromise on 2023-09-12. CoinEx noticed anomalous withdrawals from several hot wallet addresses; preliminarily it attributed them to leaked hot-wallet private keys `[securityweekcoinex]`. Assets were drained on Ether, Tron and Matic and other chains `[riskybizcoinex]`.

The loss was about \$53-54M according to tracking firms `[securityweekcoinex]` `[riskybizcoinex]`. The multi-chain pattern is consistent with leaked keys controlling hot wallets on several networks.

CoinEx moved remaining hot-wallet assets to cold storage, suspended all deposits and withdrawals, shut down the hot-wallet server, began rebuilding the wallet system, and published suspicious addresses asking projects and exchanges to help freeze funds `[securityweekcoinex]`. The cited source does not say whether CoinEx would reimburse users.

## Realised extraction

About \$53-54M across multiple chains; suspected OAK-G01. Recovery status not established in the cited sources.

## Multi-chain extraction as structural signature

The comparison below is an analytic framing, not established by the cited sources:

- **2017-2019 wave (Yapizon, Bithumb, Upbit):** single-chain or single-network extraction (Bitcoin-dominant in 2017; Ethereum-dominant by 2019). Exchange signing infrastructure was chain-isolated; the attacker had to compromise per-chain signing surfaces.
- **2022+ wave (CoinEx, Stake.com, BingX):** multi-chain simultaneous extraction. The cited sources show a single hot-wallet key leak affecting several chains; claims about pre-positioned access are unverified.

The CoinEx case is the reference for the multi-chain extraction sub-pattern within T11.001.
