# GMX V1 — re-entrancy from OrderBook into the Vault skipped the short average-price update and inflated the GLP price — GMX V1 / Arbitrum — 2025-07-09

**Loss:** **~\$42M** per [CertiK](https://www.certik.com/blog/gmx-incident-analysis) and [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/361806/hacker-drains-42-million-from-decentralized-perpetual-exchange-gmx); **~\$40M** of GLP funds on Arbitrum per [GMX](https://gmxio.substack.com/p/glp-funds-on-arbitrum-fully-recovered), and [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329821/gmx-hacker-white-hat-returns-40-million-sends-rest-tornado-cash) says about \$40M. **Returned:** per Decrypt, citing PeckShield, the exploiter returned about \$40.5M including 10,000 ETH; per CertiK about \$37M was returned, with 1,700 ETH kept as the bounty. GMX says the funds were returned after its outreach and are held by the DAO, with distribution to GLP liquidity providers pending DAO approval ([GMX](https://gmxio.substack.com/p/glp-funds-on-arbitrum-fully-recovered)). GMX V2 and the GMX token were not affected.

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.005** (Reentrancy — *primary*, operator-stated). The `nonReentrant` guard covered only functions inside the OrderBook contract, so during the ETH payout of a keeper-executed decrease order the attacker's contract re-entered the Vault's `increasePosition` directly, which skipped the average short price calculation ([GMX](https://gmxio.substack.com/p/glp-funds-on-arbitrum-fully-recovered)). See [`techniques/T9.005-reentrancy.md`](../techniques/T9.005-reentrancy.md). **OAK-T9.002** (Flash-Loan-Enabled Exploit — *secondary*, per [CertiK](https://www.certik.com/blog/gmx-incident-analysis)): about 7.538M USDC was flash-loaned to mint and stake GLP and open a large short. The earlier mapping to a legacy-version-maintenance Technique was dropped: no opened source says GMX knew of the flaw and left V1 unpatched.

**Attribution:** **pseudonymous.** CertiK and GMX call the actor a white-hat. The Block links the hacker wallet [`0xDF3340A436c27655bA62F8281565C9925C3a5221`](https://arbiscan.io/address/0xDF3340A436c27655bA62F8281565C9925C3a5221) via Arkham Intel data. No source opened names a person.

**Key teaching point:** **A reentrancy guard must cover every entry point into the state it protects, across contracts, not only the contract that holds the modifier.** Per GMX, the guard sat in OrderBook while the Vault function that mutates short size could be reached directly during the ETH payout.

## Summary

GMX V1 let users trade perpetuals against the GLP pool on Arbitrum. On 2025-07-09 the attacker opened a position from an attack contract, placed a small decrease order, and, when the keeper executed it, re-entered during the ETH payout ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/blog/gmx-incident-analysis)). Re-entering opened a WBTC short without updating the average short price; GMX gives the BTC average short price as moved from \$109,505.77 to \$1,913.70 ([GMX](https://gmxio.substack.com/p/glp-funds-on-arbitrum-fully-recovered)). That inflated the pool's assets-under-management figure and so the GLP price; the attacker then redeemed GLP at the inflated price. GMX [disabled V1 trading and GLP mint and redeem](https://www.theblock.co/post/361806/hacker-drains-42-million-from-decentralized-perpetual-exchange-gmx) on Arbitrum and Avalanche, and the attacker returned most of the funds two days later.

## Root-cause evidence

- **Technique:** OAK-T9.005
- **Source:** <https://gmxio.substack.com/p/glp-funds-on-arbitrum-fully-recovered>; <https://www.certik.com/blog/gmx-incident-analysis>
- **Source type:** operator
- **Confidence:** confirmed
- **Evidence:** GMX's own write-up says the `nonReentrant` guard covered only functions within OrderBook, so the attacker could call `increasePosition` in the Vault directly and skip the average short price calculation. CertiK, a separate firm, gives the same chain through `executeDecreaseOrder()` → `_transferOutETH()` → `_receiver.sendValue()` with `_isLeverageEnabled` still set. Sources disagree on the amount returned and on who held the 1,700 ETH; neither GMX page opened states the exact returned composition.
