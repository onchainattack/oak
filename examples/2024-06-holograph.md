# Holograph — former contractor's retained admin access to v1 contracts used to mint 1 billion HLG — Holograph / chain not stated by the main sources — 2024-06-13

**Loss:** **~\$14.4M** nominal value of **1 billion HLG** minted on 2024-06-13; HLG fell nearly 80% within nine hours, from \$0.014 to a low of \$0.0029 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). FinanceFeeds notes that the figure is the estimated value of the minted tokens, not confirmed cash from sales ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/former-holograph-employee-sentenced-to-five-years-over-14m-unauthorized-hlg-token-minting/)). Holograph said on 2024-06-14 that at least 200 million of the extra tokens were frozen, with exchange accounts frozen on Bybit, Gate, KuCoin, Bitget and Backpack, as relayed by [Odaily via Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604048977). After arrests in Italy, authorities froze and seized "significant assets and electronics", with no figure given ([Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604156634)). A French court later ordered the defendants to pay the foundation €1.15 million for direct financial losses ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/former-holograph-employee-sentenced-to-five-years-over-14m-unauthorized-hlg-token-minting/)).

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.004** (Access-Control Misconfiguration — *primary*). Per Cointelegraph, the contractor knew they had admin access to Holograph Protocol v1 contracts, and it was used as a backdoor; the article does not say how that access persisted after the contractor left ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). See [`techniques/T9.004-access-control-misconfiguration.md`](../techniques/T9.004-access-control-misconfiguration.md). **OAK-T5.003** (Hidden-Mint Dilution — the exercised mint role created 1 billion new HLG, which was then sold into the market, per [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). See [`techniques/T5.003-hidden-mint-dilution.md`](../techniques/T5.003-hidden-mint-dilution.md).

**Attribution:** **confirmed** (court conviction; the individual is not named here). Holograph's post-mortem, with Halborn assisting, blamed a disgruntled former contractor ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). Several people were arrested in Italy with French assistance, announced by Holograph in August 2024 ([Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604156634)). A Paris criminal court ruled on 2026-11-06 and convicted a former Holograph Foundation employee and two accomplices of offences including organised fraud, money laundering and unlawful interference with an automated data processing system ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/former-holograph-employee-sentenced-to-five-years-over-14m-unauthorized-hlg-token-minting/)). That article calls the defendant both a former employee and a former contractor.

**Key teaching point:** **Revoke every admin, minter and upgrade permission of a departing contributor, on every contract version still live, and alert on any mint.** The attacker was a former contractor whose admin access to the v1 contracts was still valid; Holograph afterwards said it implemented operational risk controls against insider attacks, and an outside expert recommended multisigs with well-distributed signers and prompt removal of former staff's access ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)).

## Summary

On 2024-06-13 a hacker minted 1 billion HLG, worth \$14.4M at the time, through a proxy wallet, and sold the tokens on the open market ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). Holograph's first public statement, relayed by Odaily, said a former contractor exploited a vulnerability to mint the extra tokens "on Mantle" and that the protocol was locked temporarily ([Bitget News](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604048977)); the other sources do not name a chain, so the chain is open. Holograph published a post-mortem on 2 July with Halborn's help, which blamed a disgruntled former contractor who had planned the attack months in advance ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). Holograph's own post-mortem was not opened for this rewrite.

Holograph then resumed bridging on the v2 protocol, advised exchanges to allow HLG deposits and withdrawals, and said it would burn circulating supply to cut maximum supply to 10 billion ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/holograph-token-exploit-former-contractor)). In its November 2026 report, FinanceFeeds says the former employee used prior access to Holograph's infrastructure to change token-creation permissions, minted the tokens to a wallet he controlled, and that two accomplices spread the assets across exchange accounts; the court found the later price collapse could not be reliably tied to the incident ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/former-holograph-employee-sentenced-to-five-years-over-14m-unauthorized-hlg-token-minting/)). Whether the permission change happened before or on 2024-06-13 is not stated in these sources (the FinanceFeeds article puts the activity between May and August 2024).
