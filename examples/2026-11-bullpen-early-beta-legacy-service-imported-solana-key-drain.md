# Bullpen — early-beta accounts drained through an undisclosed "legacy system", including a Solana key imported into one profile — Bullpen (trading app) / Solana, Arbitrum, Bitcoin — 2026-11-24

**Loss:** the largest single loss is the Solana wallet of the trader who posts as [@blknoiz06](https://x.com/blknoiz06) (a public pseudonymous account, cited by handle only). Bitquery reports \$2.57M in USDC reaching one new Arbitrum address; OAK's own read of Arbitrum USDC transfers into that address on 2026-11-24 (07:54–10:32 UTC) from the Relay-linked senders gives about 2,565,767 USDC (traced by OAK). Bitquery counts 14 other accounts swept by the same address on Hyperliquid, Polygon and Bitcoin, in amounts from \$1 to a few thousand dollars each; the six Hyperliquid accounts lost \$659 in total (Bitquery), and the Polygon and Bitcoin amounts are listed in the timeline. The operator counts 18–19 affected accounts and gives no loss figure. No freeze or recovery is reported by any source read. The memecoin sales that funded the drain also moved the ANSEM token price: Bitquery reports a 57% fall.

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.013** (Legacy-Version Maintenance Attack Surface — *primary, operator-stated entry, partial fit*). Bullpen says the attacker "got in through a legacy system tied to his account and the accounts of 18 other early beta testers" and that it shut the system down. T11.013 is written for protocol versions that stay deployed after their successor ships. Here the operator names a legacy service in an off-chain trading app, and what the service was is **undisclosed**, so the fit is by the control (decommission what is no longer maintained), not by a confirmed mechanism. See [`techniques/T11.013-legacy-version-maintenance-attack-surface.md`](../techniques/T11.013-legacy-version-maintenance-attack-surface.md). **OAK-T11.012** (Server-side Raw Private-Key Storage — *secondary, partial fit*). The account owner says his Solana private key had been imported into his Bullpen profile, so the platform held key material that could sign for him. Whether the key was stored raw, encrypted, or signed through another path, and where, is **undisclosed**; the pattern (many accounts opened in one period, drained by one party) is consistent with a shared key store but no source states one. See [`techniques/T11.012-server-side-raw-private-key-storage.md`](../techniques/T11.012-server-side-raw-private-key-storage.md). **OAK-T7.003** (Cross-chain Bridge Laundering — *laundering step*: Solana to Arbitrum through Relay, then USDC to Bitcoin through Bridgers, per Bitquery and OAK's trace). No entry Technique from T15.x is claimed: no source says how the attacker reached the legacy system.

**Attribution:** **unattributed.** No source names an actor. Bitquery states that its labels do not name the owner of an older Solana wallet that was emptied the same morning and sent the first \$27 to the same Arbitrum address, and it attributes its wallets to nobody. Whether the attacker was an outside party is disputed by some observers (see "Disputed point" below); that dispute is reported, not resolved.

**Key teaching point:** **A service the operator no longer runs for users is still a signing path if it can use keys users gave it; decommission it, or delete the key material from it, before it becomes the only unmonitored path to key access.** This is a control statement drawn from the operator's own account (a "legacy system" that was shut down once found), not a published finding about how the system worked.

## Summary

On 2026-11-24 the account owner posted at 10:59 UTC that his "pump wallet is hacked" and that he believed it was "unauthorized access to my bullpen profile somehow which had the sol priv key imported". Lookonchain had already posted at 09:55 UTC that the funds from memecoin sales were going to the Arbitrum address `0xAC33` (0xAC33748aAd09CEA8E50484Cc0Fa168dc23531B4d). Bitquery's trace shows the Solana wallet first sent \$515K USDC through new addresses at 06:46 UTC, sold about \$2.1M of memecoins from 08:30 to 11:00, and sent 29,104 SOL plus the \$515K through Relay, which paid out USDC to one Arbitrum address in 28 payments, \$2.57M by 10:33. At 16:35 UTC Bullpen said it had identified unauthorized transfers on accounts opened in its April–June 2024 early beta, preliminary count 18–19, and had taken "the affected test service" offline. On 2026-11-25 at 13:40 UTC it said the attacker "got in through a legacy system tied to his account and the accounts of 18 other early beta testers", that it shut the system down, and that no other Bullpen accounts were affected. That evening the Arbitrum address swapped the USDC through Bridgers into 42.24 BTC across four new Bitcoin addresses. OAK found those four addresses consolidated into one Taproot address on 2026-11-25 between 16:47 and 17:11 UTC, after Bitquery's 14:40 UTC cutoff.

## Disputed point

RektRadar reports that some on-chain observers noted activity patterns that "did not match typical external drain signatures in every detail", including earlier small inflows, and that some community analysis questions a pure external-hack explanation. RektRadar attributes this to unnamed observers and does not name a source. Bitquery reports that one analyst had read the older wallet that sent the first \$27 as a sign of an insider, says the account owner's statement makes that less likely, and says "Bullpen's own statement, and the afternoon drains below, raise our confidence that this is a theft to high". The account owner wrote on 2026-11-24 at 13:41 UTC "it is not me", in a reply whose context OAK did not read. Bullpen's statement describes the incident as a "targeted attack on Ansem". OAK records the disagreement and takes no side; no source read establishes who operated the attacker address.

## What is not known yet

- What the "legacy system" was (a service, a database, an admin tool, a vendor, an access path), how it was reached, and how imported keys were stored (raw, encrypted, or signed through another path): **undisclosed**. Bullpen's two posts do not say.
- Whether the key was taken from the platform or from elsewhere: the account owner states he does not know the vector; the chain cannot show how the key leaked (Bitquery).
- Total loss across all affected accounts: Bullpen gives no figure. Bitquery found 14 other paying accounts and says Bullpen's count of 18–19 is not settled by the chain (some accounts may have held nothing or held assets on chains it did not check).
- Who owns the Ethereum wallet that sent \$1.58M to the attacker address, and whether it was a victim.
- Whether any funds can be recovered: Bitquery found USDC freezable only until 17:49 UTC; the account owner said a court order was required. By 2026-11-25 17:11 UTC the Bitcoin had been consolidated; the Ethereum holdings were not re-read.
- Whether the Solana-side steps match Bitquery's account: OAK's Solana read returned no transfers for the window, so those steps are relayed only.
