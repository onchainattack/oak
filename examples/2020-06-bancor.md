# Bancor v0.6 BancorNetwork exposed-approval vulnerability — Ethereum — 2020-06-18

**Loss:** approximately \$455K of user funds were at risk and were moved by Bancor in a white-hat rescue; two arbitrage bots front-ran the rescue transactions and captured about \$135K `[theblock1]`. Decrypt reported about \$410K rescued by Bancor, about \$132K taken by a "friendly" front-runner and just over \$3K by another `[decrypt1]`. Return of the front-runners' funds was unconfirmed at the time of reporting `[theblock1]``[decrypt1]`.
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.004** (Access-Control Misconfiguration — the v0.6 contracts "mistakenly made a safeTransferFrom function in the BancorNetwork contract public", letting anyone move tokens that users had approved to the contract `[theblock1]`). **OAK-T16.006** (Timelock-Free Protocol Upgrade Execution — retained from the original classification; the cited sources do not describe the upgrade process, so this mapping is unverified).
**Attribution:** **pseudonymous** — the actors were Bancor's own rescue team and two automated front-running bots; no malicious exploitation was reported `[decrypt1]`.
**Key teaching point:** **A routine contract upgrade that accidentally exposed a transfer function turned every outstanding token approval to the BancorNetwork contract into a liability; users who had traded in the previous 48 hours were at risk until they revoked approvals.** The case also shows that a white-hat rescue is visible in the public mempool and can be front-run by bots.

**Corrected 2026-10-11:** The original described a reserve-accounting logic bug in BancorConverter drained by an unnamed white-hat researcher who returned \$460K (12.5 ETH + ~229K USDT) within 24 hours; sources instead describe a publicly exposed `safeTransferFrom` in BancorNetwork v0.6 exploiting user approvals, a Bancor-run rescue, and front-running bots. The researcher, v0.6.1 patch, fund-return and 2020-06-20 post-mortem details were removed as unsupported.

## Summary

Bancor deployed v0.6 of its BancorNetwork contracts on 2020-06-16. The contracts "mistakenly made a safeTransferFrom function in the BancorNetwork contract public", so anyone could use users' existing token approvals to move their tokens `[theblock1]``[decrypt1]`. Bancor discovered the problem around midnight GMT into 2020-06-18 and warned its community `[decrypt1]`.

Bancor ran a white-hat operation, moving roughly \$410K-\$455K of exposed user funds to a safe wallet in batched transactions; two arbitrage bots detected the pending transactions and front-ran them, earning about \$135K `[theblock1]``[decrypt1]`. Bancor deployed a patched network contract and told users who had traded in the previous 48 hours to revoke their approvals to the old contract `[theblock1]``[decrypt1]`. Bancor contacted the bot owners seeking return of funds in exchange for a bug bounty `[theblock1]`.

This is distinct from the July 2018 Bancor compromise (see [`examples/2018-07-bancor.md`](../examples/2018-07-bancor.md)), which was a custody-layer incident.

## Realised extraction

About \$135K was captured by two front-running bots per The Block; Decrypt gave about \$135K combined (\$132K + \$3K). Whether it was returned is not established in the cited sources `[theblock1]``[decrypt1]`.
