# @ape_31 — fake Bybit demo PNL screenshots sold copy-trading that did not exist — X / exchange deposit addresses — 2023-2024

**Loss:** **\$650K+** from **250+** people over about a year, per [ZachXBT's thread](https://x.com/zachxbt/status/1840749550304125401). [Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/14253557568297) relays that fees started at \$250 to \$500 and that the scammer used more than 16 deposit wallets. [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/uk-based-scammer-stole-650k-in-12-months/) relays individual losses of up to \$40,000.

**OAK Techniques observed:** none — advance-fee service fraud: fabricated Bybit demo-account PNL screenshots, a paid copy-trading service that did not exist, a fake API key, then escalating demands and blocking ([Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/14253557568297)). It is not a wallet drain, and no opened page describes custody of victim funds or a Ponzi payout structure, so no listed Technique fits.

**Attribution:** **pseudonymous** — the handles @ape_31 and @60711 on X; ZachXBT says the operator is UK-based ([thread](https://x.com/zachxbt/status/1840749550304125401)). [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/uk-based-scammer-stole-650k-in-12-months/) relays that a TradingView account showed a UK telecom provider. No person is named by any opened page.

**Key teaching point:** **Treat posted PNL screenshots as untrusted: a Bybit demo account produces them.** Nobody who is making large profits sells copy-trading for \$250 ([ZachXBT, 2023-09-28](https://x.com/zachxbt/status/1707543143799816518), about the similar handle @Ape31111).

## Summary

On 2024-09-30 [ZachXBT wrote](https://x.com/zachxbt/status/1840749550304125401) that the UK scammer @ape_31 / @60711 had taken more than \$650K from 250+ X users in the past year. He did it by posting fake PNL screenshots from a Bybit demo account and running a news account to funnel victims into paid services, then blocking them on payment. [Binance Square](https://www.binance.com/en/square/post/14253557568297) relays further details: fees of \$250 to \$500; one victim who paid \$500 was asked for \$20,000 to trade at a zero liquidation price (relayed) and then for another \$20,000; one victim got an API key to monitor trades that were fake; another sent \$3,000 after a promise of growth from \$10,000 to \$100,000. The scammer rotated deposit wallets.
