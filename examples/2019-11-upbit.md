# Upbit exchange hack — Ethereum — 2019-11-27

**Loss:** 342,000 ETH, about \$48.7M (58 billion won) at the time per Upbit's CEO, from South Korean exchange Upbit's Ethereum hot wallet, moved in one transfer to an unknown address `[securityaffairs]``[theblock]`. Reports give \$41.5M to ~\$50M for the 2019 value depending on the price used.
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.001** (Third-Party Signing-Vendor UI / Signing-Flow Compromise — broadly construed; the access path was never published, so the exact mechanism is unverified). **OAK-T7.001** (Mixer-Routed Laundering — per South Korean police, about 57% of the ETH was converted to bitcoin through three online exchanges and the rest sent to 51 overseas exchanges `[theblock]`).
**OAK-Gnn:** [OAK-G01 Lazarus Group / DPRK-attributed](../actors/OAK-G01-lazarus.md).
**Attribution:** **confirmed** — on 2024-11-21 South Korea's National Police Agency said North Korean hackers were responsible, citing North Korean IP addresses, asset flows, North Korean terminology, and FBI cooperation `[theblock]`; Korean coverage names the Lazarus and Andariel groups `[cointelegraphkr]`.
**Key teaching point:** **Upbit's 342,000 ETH single-transfer hot-wallet drain was attributed to North Korea only five years later, by police evidence rather than on-chain clustering alone.** It illustrates the long gap between an exchange hot-wallet theft and a formal state attribution.

**Corrected 2026-10-11:** Attribution source corrected to the National Police Agency statement of 2024-11-21 (not a "Cyber Bureau" or wallet-cluster vendors); laundering description replaced with police-reported figures; unsourced claims (audit, resumption dates, DPRK-pattern comparisons, Chainalysis/Elliptic/TRM and UN Panel citations) removed; 2019 value range noted.

## Summary

Upbit, a South Korean exchange operated by Dunamu, reported that at 1:06 PM on 2019-11-27, 342,000 ETH was moved from its Ethereum hot wallet to an unknown address `[securityaffairs]`. Upbit's CEO valued it at about 58 billion won (\$48.7M), said the exchange would cover user losses from its own assets, suspended deposits and withdrawals, and moved its crypto holdings to cold wallets `[securityaffairs]`. The exchange stopped short of calling the incident a "hack" in its first statements `[securityaffairs]`.

On 2024-11-21 the National Police Agency announced that North Korea was the perpetrator, citing North Korean IP addresses, the flow of virtual assets and terminology, and long-term FBI cooperation; the statement called the victim only "A", which police confirmed to be Upbit `[theblock]`. About 57% of the ETH was converted to bitcoin at a 2.5% discount through three online exchanges that appear to have been attacker-created; the rest went to 51 overseas exchanges. Police working with Swiss authorities recovered 4.8 BTC and returned it to Upbit `[theblock]`.
