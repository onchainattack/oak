The ~\$18M figure that appeared in earlier text is the estimated value of the 29,657 BTC lot sold at the 2014-06-27 U.S. Marshals auction, not of the 144,000 BTC seizure `[slkroadwiki]`. The later-price and "largest seizure" claims are unsourced and removed.
**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T7.001** (Mixer-Routed Hop — earlier text described an internal "tumbler" routing every withdrawal; the cited sources do not establish this and it should be treated as unverified. Sales through the site were about \$1.2B between 2011-02-06 and 2013-07-23 per the server image `[slkroadwiki]`). **OAK-T3.001** (Sybil-Bundled Launch — broadly construed for the social-platform distribution primitive; Silk Road's Tor-hidden-service marketplace was a large-scale darknet-marketplace aggregation surface).
**Attribution:** **confirmed**. Arrested by FBI agents on 2013-10-01 at the Glen Park branch of the San Francisco Public Library `[slkroadwiki]`. Convicted on 2015-02-04 on seven charges in federal court in Manhattan; sentenced 2015-05-29 to two life sentences without parole (five concurrent sentences) plus a \$183 million forfeiture; pardoned by President Trump on 2025-01-21 `[slkroadwiki]`.
**Key teaching point:** **Silk Road is the canonical 2011-2013 worked example of Bitcoin as a medium-of-exchange for an illicit-activity marketplace at scale and the first major US law-enforcement cryptocurrency-seizure operation. The case established the operational template for darknet-marketplace takedowns (server-seizure at the hosting layer, wallet-seizure via defendant-side endpoint access, blockchain-forensic reconstruction of the operator's financial flows) and demonstrated that Bitcoin's pseudonymity could be penetrated through a combination of traditional law-enforcement investigation and endpoint-side compromise — the operational ancestor of modern blockchain-forensic investigation methodology.**

## Summary

Silk Road operated as a Tor-hidden-service darknet marketplace from February 2011 until the FBI takedown on 2013-10-01. The platform facilitated the sale of narcotics and other illicit goods exclusively in Bitcoin, with the operator ("Dread Pirate Roberts" / Ross Ulbricht) collecting commissions. The server image shows about 1,229,465 transactions, 146,946 buyers and 3,877 vendors between 2011-02-06 and 2013-07-23, roughly \$1.2 billion in sales `[slkroadwiki]`.

Bitcoin was the sole payment rail. Earlier text here asserted a platform-integrated tumbler that mixed all withdrawals; that is not established by the sources cited here.

The FBI investigation culminated in the 2013-10-01 arrest of Ulbricht in San Francisco and seizure of his laptop; the FBI initially seized about 26,000 BTC from Silk Road accounts and later reported seizing 144,000 BTC it said belonged to Ulbricht `[slkroadwiki]``[slkroadforbes]`. How the FBI located the server is covered in `[slkroadwired]`; claims here about hosting country and server-compromise methodology are unverified.

The U.S. Marshals Service sold 29,657 BTC in 10 blocks on 2014-06-27 (estimated \$18 million); Tim Draper bought them `[slkroadwiki]``[slkroaddraper]`. Claims that this was the first government cryptocurrency auction are unverified.
