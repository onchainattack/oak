# Crédit Mutuel Arkéa — attackers reached the bank's own account through a service provider's platform and wired about \$133M to crypto exchange accounts, then withdrew stablecoins — Crédit Mutuel Arkéa (French bank) / Ethereum and exchange accounts — 2026-11-01

**Loss:** **~\$133M+** (about 118M euros) per ZachXBT's on-chain count of stablecoin withdrawals from exchange accounts, which he first put at \$106M+ (2026-11-02) and raised by \$27M+ (2026-11-03). The bank does not confirm the figure but "validates the order of magnitude" (Clubic), and says the amount is still being assessed. Reuters, relayed by Security Affairs, gave "around €100 million (\$112.61 million)". The figures are on different bases and are not reconciled. Kraken restricted the linked accounts and Crypto.com blocked the related funds (Clubic, citing ZachXBT), and the bank says part of the funds was blocked. ZachXBT counts more than \$30M already laundered by 2026-11-03. The bank says customer accounts and assets were not touched; it was its own account.

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T15.002** (Supply-Chain / Vendor-Pipeline Compromise — *closest fit, inferred, mechanism undisclosed*. The bank says the attackers "passed through the platform of a service provider and directly reached Crédit Mutuel Arkéa", not through its subsidiary Fortuneo, and used the bank's own account to send payments. Whether the provider platform was compromised, abused with stolen credentials or used as designed is not published. See [`techniques/T15.002-supply-chain-vendor-pipeline-compromise.md`](../techniques/T15.002-supply-chain-vendor-pipeline-compromise.md)). **OAK-T7.003** (Cross-chain Bridge Laundering — *laundering step*: ZachXBT reports "bridging across multiple chains". See [`techniques/T7.003-cross-chain-bridge-laundering.md`](../techniques/T7.003-cross-chain-bridge-laundering.md)). **OAK-T7.005** (Privacy-chain Hops — *laundering step*: USDC swapped for ETH, sent to instant exchanges (WellWallet, Kyrrex) and converted to XMR through Wagyu, per ZachXBT. See [`techniques/T7.005-privacy-chain-hops.md`](../techniques/T7.005-privacy-chain-hops.md)).

**Attribution:** **unattributed.** ZachXBT: "While the illicit actor remains unidentified, I can say with a high degree of confidence it is NOT Lazarus Group based on multiple laundering indicators." The Paris prosecutor's cybercrime unit opened an investigation after the bank's complaint.

**Key teaching point:** **A bank's own account is only as safe as the weakest platform that can instruct it.** Payments from the bank's treasury account reached exchange accounts and were out as stablecoins within hours, and the bank's own statement says its controls spotted the activity quickly, but only after the money had moved. A payer-side control that no provider session can satisfy alone (a beneficiary allowlist for the own-account, a second approval from a separate system for new beneficiaries and for crypto-exchange beneficiaries) is the step that breaks this chain. That is OAK's reading of the stated facts; the bank has not published which control failed.

## Summary

On 2026-11-01 stablecoins worth \$106M+ were withdrawn from several centralized exchange accounts. ZachXBT flagged it on 2026-11-02 as an unnamed EU entity, raised it to \$133M+ on 11-03 with the working theory that "a large financial institution in Western Europe was exploited and it's currently being covered up", and on 11-04 named Crédit Mutuel Arkéa, "allegedly". The bank confirmed a cyberattack to AFP on 11-04 and published a statement that evening: attackers used a service provider's platform to reach the bank's own account and sent payments to other banking actors, which "potentially" let them convert the money to crypto through specialised platforms; no customer account or subsidiary was used; the incident is "contained". Secondary press (dcod, citing Presse-citron) adds that the payments went to exchange accounts opened under false identities; the bank has not said so. The provider is not named and the technical access is undisclosed.

## What is not known yet

- How the attackers got into the provider platform and which provider it is: **undisclosed**. Press reports that the payments started at Fortuneo are denied by the bank.
- How the payments left the bank (which rail, which beneficiary banks) and whether the exchange accounts were opened under false identities: the bank says only "other banking actors" and "specialised platforms"; the false-identity claim is secondary (dcod).
- Final loss: ~\$133M+ (ZachXBT), about 118M euros (Le Télégramme via Clubic) or about €100M (Reuters via Security Affairs). The bank has not published a figure.
- Which exchanges held the accounts, beyond Kraken and Crypto.com acting on linked accounts, and who operates the common source contract: not identified by OAK.
- What was recovered: the bank says part was blocked, with no amount.
