# Hedgey Finance — createLockedCampaign() approval not revoked by cancelCampaign() — Hedgey ClaimCampaigns / Ethereum and Arbitrum — 2024-04-19

**Loss:** Cointelegraph, relaying two Cyvers alerts, reports \$44.7M in two parallel exploits: over \$42.8M on Arbitrum and \$1.9M on Ethereum ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit)). CertiK disputes the headline figure: it says the roughly \$44M circulating in reports came from multiplying the stolen BONUS amount by its token price, which ignores the token's limited liquidity, and puts the loss at about \$2M on Ethereum plus a quantity of BONUS tokens on Arbitrum ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). The first exploit took 1.3M USDC, and NOBL tokens taken were sold for about \$600K ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). The sources opened report no recovery ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit)).

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T9.016** (Contract Accounting / State-Invariant Flaw — *primary*). CertiK describes `createLockedCampaign()` granting an approval to the `tokenLocker`, which in the attack was the exploiter's own contract; `cancelCampaign()` moved the tokens to the `tokenLocker` but did not revoke that approval, so the attacker could then call `transferFrom` on the approved tokens ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). See [`techniques/T9.016-contract-accounting-state-invariant-flaw.md`](../techniques/T9.016-contract-accounting-state-invariant-flaw.md). **OAK-T9.002** (Flash-Loan-Enabled Exploit — *enabling*). In the first transaction the attacker flash-loaned 1.305M USDC from Balancer ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). See [`techniques/T9.002-flash-loan-enabled-exploit.md`](../techniques/T9.002-flash-loan-enabled-exploit.md). **OAK-T7.002** (CEX Deposit-Address Layering — *secondary*). Part of the Arbitrum proceeds went to Bybit ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit)); CertiK counts about 200K BONUS sent to a Bybit account ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). See [`techniques/T7.002-cex-deposit-layering.md`](../techniques/T7.002-cex-deposit-layering.md).

**Attribution:** **unattributed**. CertiK identifies three separate exploiters (a main attacker on Ethereum and two copycats) and does not name any person; it links one copycat's funding address to an earlier exploit of an old Kardia Bridge approval contract ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). Cointelegraph attributes the attack to no group or individual ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit)).

**Key teaching point:** **When a function hands out a token approval, every path that ends the arrangement must revoke it.** CertiK calls the cause "A single line of missing code to revoke user's approvals" ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). Test each approval-granting function against its cancel and refund paths, and check that no allowance to a caller-chosen address survives them.

## Summary

Hedgey Finance's token-claim contract `ClaimCampaigns.sol` is at [`0xBc452fdC8F851d7c5B72e1Fe74DFB63bb793D511`](https://etherscan.io/address/0xBc452fdC8F851d7c5B72e1Fe74DFB63bb793D511) per CertiK ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). On 2024-04-19 Cyvers alerted on two exploits, and Hedgey confirmed an attack on its Token Claim Contract and told users with active claims to cancel them with the "End Token Claim" button ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit)). Scam accounts impersonating Hedgey then posted malicious links under its thread ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit)).

CertiK's analysis: the attacker flash-loaned USDC, created a locked campaign naming the attacker's contract as the `tokenLocker`, cancelled it, and then used the surviving approval to `transferFrom` the tokens held by the campaign contract ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). The attacker repeated the process to take NOBL tokens ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)). CertiK found two further exploiters who copied the technique, one of whom took 78,148,820 BONUS tokens on Arbitrum ([CertiK](https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis)).

## Root-cause evidence

- **Technique:** OAK-T9.016
- **Source:** <https://www.certik.com/resources/blog/hedgey-finance-incident-analysis>; <https://cointelegraph.com/news/hedgey-protocol-44-million-exploit>
- **Source type:** security-firm
- **Confidence:** reported
- **Evidence:** CertiK's analysis traces the mechanism to two transactions and two functions. Cointelegraph relays only the Cyvers alerts and Hedgey's own statement, which says "an attack on the Hedgey Token Claim Contract" and names no mechanism. No operator post-mortem was opened, so the mechanism rests on one security firm. The loss figure is disputed (\$44.7M per Cointelegraph, about \$2M plus illiquid BONUS per CertiK). Which transactions the \$1.9M Ethereum figure and the Arbitrum figure correspond to is not stated.
