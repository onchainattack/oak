# Infini — former developer's retained admin rights used to move \$49.5M USDC out of the neobank's contract — Infini / Ethereum — 2025-02-24

**Loss:** about \$49.5M in USDC on 2025-02-24 ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158), citing PeckShield). The legal notice Infini served on-chain cites 49,516,662.977 USDC ([CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/infini-files-suit-after-50m-theft-issues-blockchain-legal-notice/)). **Recovery:** none reported. Infini offered the attacker a 20% bounty for returning the rest, then offered to stop pursuit if 80% was returned to its Cobo custodian wallet; the accused did not respond and no funds were reported returned ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158); [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/infini-files-suit-after-50m-theft-issues-blockchain-legal-notice/); [FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/infini-sues-former-developer-others-over-50m-crypto-hack-in-hong-kong/)). The founder said he would cover losses from personal funds ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158)).

**OAK Techniques observed:** **OAK-T11.014** (Operator Privileged-Key Compromise — *primary*). TokenPost describes admin rights over Infini's smart contract that a developer retained, used to drain the funds months later ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158)). The privileged authority was exercised as the contract allowed. See [`techniques/T11.014-operator-privileged-key-compromise.md`](../techniques/T11.014-operator-privileged-key-compromise.md). Funding the exploit wallet through Tornado Cash is a preparation step ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/infini-sues-former-developer-others-over-50m-crypto-hack-in-hong-kong/)); no laundering Technique is mapped because the sources do not trace onward movement beyond the swap to ETH.

**Attribution:** **inferred-strong, by the victim and a monitoring firm.** Cyvers reported a former Infini developer was responsible ([CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/infini-files-suit-after-50m-theft-issues-blockchain-legal-notice/)). Infini's parent company filed a civil suit in Hong Kong against a former developer and unidentified others; no arrest or criminal charge is reported in the sources read ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/infini-sues-former-developer-others-over-50m-crypto-hack-in-hong-kong/); [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/infini-files-suit-after-50m-theft-issues-blockchain-legal-notice/)). This file does not name the defendant.

**Key teaching point:** **When a developer leaves, revoke their on-chain admin role and rotate every credential for the production contract in the same step, and enforce it on-chain rather than by process.** TokenPost describes the cause as admin rights a developer retained over the contract ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158)).

## Summary

On 2025-02-24 about \$49.5M in USDC left Infini, a Hong Kong-based stablecoin neobank, days after it announced \$50M in total value locked ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158)). Cyvers first flagged unauthorised transfers and then said a former Infini developer was responsible ([CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/infini-files-suit-after-50m-theft-issues-blockchain-legal-notice/)). The attacker funded an exploit wallet with 1 ETH through Tornado Cash and withdrew the USDC through a contract deployed in November 2024, then swapped it to DAI and converted it to 17,696 ETH moved to a secondary address ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/infini-sues-former-developer-others-over-50m-crypto-hack-in-hong-kong/)). Infini messaged the attacker on-chain, saying it had collected IP and device data, and offered a 20% bounty with a 48-hour deadline ([TokenPost](https://www.tokenpost.com/news/business/14158)). In March 2025 it obtained a Hong Kong injunction served on-chain to the linked wallets ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/infini-sues-former-developer-others-over-50m-crypto-hack-in-hong-kong/)).
