# OAK-T16.003 — Delegation-Cluster Vote Takeover (DEPRECATED)

**Maturity:** deprecated
**Superseded by:** [OAK-T16.002](./T16.002-hostile-vote-treasury-drain.md) for the one real incident once filed here (Compound Proposal 289); the delegation-cluster shape itself is returned to [`TAXONOMY-GAPS.md`](../TAXONOMY-GAPS.md) as a candidate
**Deprecated:** 2026-11-27

## Why this ID is deprecated

The Technique described voting power gathered by **delegation from a cluster of wallets to one delegate**, held for weeks, and then used to pass a hostile proposal. The 2026-11-27 source pass found no documented incident of that shape.

- **Compound Proposal 289 (2024-07-28)**, the canonical anchor. The Block reports, relaying Compound's security advisor, that the bloc bought COMP on the open market; the proposal passed the vote and was cancelled by agreement, not stopped in the timelock window as this page said. It is filed under OAK-T16.002 with confidence `disputed`: see [`examples/2024-07-compound-vote-takeover.md`](../examples/2024-07-compound-vote-takeover.md).
- **"Humpy" at Balancer (2022)**, listed as an antecedent. Decrypt describes BAL bought and locked for veBAL, with no delegation.
- **"Humpy" at SushiSwap (2024)**, listed as an antecedent. The only delegation in the sources is an allegation by Sushi's head chef that one holder delegated part of its tokens to a group of former contributors, which the holder disputes. That is one holder delegating out, not a cluster delegating in.
- **SushiSwap 2025, Balancer veBAL 2023–2024 and Gitcoin 2023 "delegation takeover" examples**: no public record; quarantined (see `CORRECTIONS.md`).
- **ENS DAO and Uniswap DAO "delegation concentration" examples**: the opened sources describe concentrated voting power and, for Uniswap, ten wallets funded by token transfer from one wallet. Neither has a hostile proposal or a loss; both were quarantined on 2026-11-27.
- **Beanstalk (2022-04)**, listed as a "flash-loan sub-shape": it is the canonical OAK-T16.001 case and stays there.

With no incident behind it, the ID does not meet OAK's bar. The ID is retained and not reused, per `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## What is real nearby

- **Buying votes on the open market and passing a self-serving treasury proposal** is OAK-T16.002 (Hostile-Vote Treasury Drain).
- **Borrowing votes for one block** is OAK-T16.001 (Vote Takeover via Flash-Loan).
- **Standing concentration of delegated votes** (a few delegates able to reach quorum) is a governance design risk. It is not an attack until someone uses it, and OAK records attacks. If an incident is documented in which delegation gathered from many wallets carried a hostile proposal, it goes to TAXONOMY-GAPS as a candidate and is minted under a new ID.

## What went wrong, for the record

The page, its spec and its examples were written in the v0.3 bulk additions around one real event, Compound Proposal 289, described from memory of its headlines. The delegation mechanism, the timelock-window defence and the cross-protocol "pattern of conduct" were added without a source, and three more examples were written to fit the page. None was checked against a source until the 2026-11 passes. This is the same failure as OAK-T9.012 and OAK-T10.008.
