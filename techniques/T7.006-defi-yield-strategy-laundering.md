# OAK-T7.006 — DeFi Yield-Strategy Laundering (DEPRECATED)

**Maturity:** deprecated
**Superseded by:** none — a real neighbouring pattern is returned to [`TAXONOMY-GAPS.md`](../TAXONOMY-GAPS.md) as a candidate
**Deprecated:** 2026-11-27

## Why this ID is deprecated

The Technique described stolen funds **parked in lending, staking or yield protocols** so that the position looks like an ordinary yield user's and the protocol's pooled funds break the trail. It was rated `stable`. The 2026-11-27 source passes found no incident of that shape.

- Its four worked examples (three yield-laundering files and a laundering-infrastructure cohort) rested on one Chainalysis page, [2024 crypto money laundering](https://www.chainalysis.com/blog/2024-crypto-money-laundering/). That page reports mixer inflows falling and bridge inflows rising, does not mention lending or yield protocols, and says that DeFi's transparency generally makes it a poor choice for obfuscating the movement of funds. All four were quarantined (see `CORRECTIONS.md`).
- The claim that the Lazarus Group is the main user of yield-protocol cover is on no opened page.
- A search for a named case (Aave, Compound, Lido, Curve, liquid staking; Chainalysis, TRM and Elliptic write-ups) found none.

With no documented incident behind it, the ID does not meet OAK's bar. The ID is retained and not reused, per `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## What is real nearby

- **Borrowing against stolen collateral.** After the Binance theft of 2026-10-29, Elliptic reports that an attacker wallet deposited 59.9 million USDT into Aave as collateral and borrowed 19,762 ETH before Aave froze the markets ([Elliptic](https://www.elliptic.co/insights/binance-largest-hack-in-history/); [`examples/2026-10-binance-devops-laptop-service-container-hot-wallet-drain.md`](../examples/2026-10-binance-devops-laptop-service-container-hot-wallet-drain.md)). CoinDesk reports the same move with unbacked rsETH after the KelpDAO exploit ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/20/a-usd300m-borrowing-spike-on-aave-signals-liquidity-crunch-after-exploit)). This turns an asset an issuer can freeze, or one with no backing, into one nobody can freeze, and leaves the lender with the loss. It is not yield cover: the position is opened at once and left behind. It is recorded in TAXONOMY-GAPS as a candidate Technique.
- **Mixers, bridges and instant exchanges** carry the laundering that the sources do document: OAK-T7.001, OAK-T7.003 and OAK-T7.005.

## What went wrong, for the record

The page was written from the idea that sanctions on Tornado Cash must have pushed launderers into yield protocols, and cited a report that says something else. Detection thresholds (lock-up minimums, "no yield claimed", protocol counts) were added without a source, and the `stable` rating was never tested against an incident. Same failure as OAK-T9.012, OAK-T10.008, OAK-T16.003 and OAK-T17.005.
