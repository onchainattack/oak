# OAK-T12.003 — Royalty Bypass / Marketplace Manipulation (DEPRECATED)

**Maturity:** deprecated
**Superseded by:** none — a marketplace's royalty policy is not an attack; NFT wash trading stays under [OAK-T12.001](./T12.001-nft-wash-trade-volume-inflation.md)
**Deprecated:** 2026-11-28

## Why this ID is deprecated

The Technique described NFT trades that settle without paying the creator's stated royalty. Its own page said the "bypass" was "a marketplace contract executing exactly as designed" and "more industry policy problem than exploit". The 2026-11-28 check found no incident in which anyone was attacked.

- **The X2Y2 and LooksRare royalty-optional shift (2022)**, **the "2024 royalty-enforcement sunset"** and **the NFT wrapper cohort (NFTX, FloorDAO, Sudoswap)**, its three royalty-only examples. The first two are marketplaces announcing a fee policy. The third is a side-effect of wrapping an NFT into a fungible token, and its own text said the wrapper protocols "are not attackers". All three also carried errors: the X2Y2 file left out that X2Y2 returned to enforcing royalties on 2022-11-18; the 2024 file named no 2024 event and gave a loss of "hundreds of millions of dollars" that none of its sources carries; every reference in the wrapper file was marked "(proposed)" and had no URL. Quarantined.
- A search for a circumvented royalty *enforcement mechanism* (OpenSea's Operator Filter Registry, ERC-721C transfer validators, Metaplex rule sets, Magic Eden's Open Creator Protocol) or for stolen royalty proceeds found one borderline event and no attack; see below.

ERC-2981 tells a marketplace what royalty a creator asks for and leaves payment to the marketplace. A venue that does not pay it breaks no control and takes nothing from a wallet. With no documented incident behind it, the ID does not meet OAK's bar. The ID is retained and not reused, per `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## What is real nearby

- **Wash trading for marketplace token rewards** is real and is OAK-T12.001.
- **One venue routing around another venue's blocklist.** On 2023-01-31 a news report, which said it rested on unconfirmed posts, described Blur listing collections that OpenSea's Operator Filter Registry was meant to block, through a Seaport-based contract that was not on the list: <https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/blur-reportedly-finds-loophole-openseas-210802352.html>. OpenSea stopped enforcing the filter on 2023-08-31: <https://cointelegraph.com/news/opensea-disable-on-chain-royalty-enforcement-tool>. This is a commercial dispute between marketplaces over a fee, with no victim wallet and no stolen asset.

If an incident is documented in which a royalty enforcement contract was exploited, or royalty proceeds were redirected to an attacker, it goes to TAXONOMY-GAPS as a candidate. Redirected proceeds would most likely fit an existing access-control or key-compromise Technique.

## What went wrong, for the record

The page was written from the design of ERC-2981 and from the 2022 royalty debate, and said from the start that it was a policy question. Examples were then attached that were essays on that debate. Same failure as OAK-T9.012, OAK-T10.008, OAK-T16.003, OAK-T17.005, OAK-T7.006, OAK-T10.004, OAK-T16.004, OAK-T7.010 and OAK-T10.006.
