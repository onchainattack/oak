# OAK-T9.016 — Contract Accounting / State-Invariant Flaw

**Parent Tactics:** OAK-T9 (Smart-Contract Exploit)
**Adjacent Techniques:** OAK-T9.004 (Access-Control Misconfiguration — the sibling: there, someone acted who should not have been allowed to act; here, the caller was entitled to the call and the contract's bookkeeping was wrong), OAK-T9.011 (Precision-Loss / Rounding Attack — the arithmetic sub-case where the error is rounding direction under integer division), OAK-T9.005 (Reentrancy — state read mid-update by a re-entering caller), OAK-T9.001 (Oracle Price Manipulation — the attacker moved an external price; here the contract mis-states its own internal value)
**Maturity:** emerging
**Chains:** chain-agnostic (EVM, Solana, Move-family, Starknet — the defect is in contract logic, not the runtime)
**First documented:** 2020-era DeFi at the class level; earliest corpus anchors Uranium Finance (2021-04, swap-invariant constant error) and Compound (2021-09, Comptroller distribution bug)
**Aliases:** "logic flaw", "accounting bug", "state-machine bug", "invariant violation", "missing solvency check", "contract-correctness bug", "business-logic exploit"

## Description

The contract lets a legitimate caller perform a legitimate call, and the call leaves the contract's books wrong in the caller's favour. No role is bypassed and no check of *who* is asking fails. What fails is the contract's model of *how much* — a balance, a reserve, a share price, a debt position, a claim record, a state flag — so that the attacker ends the transaction owed, or holding, more than they put in.

T9.016 exists because OAK previously filed these cases under T9.004 (Access-Control Misconfiguration), which described itself as "the residual category" for logic flaws. That grouping made the corpus's most-cited Technique mean two different things and pointed defenders at the wrong control: an access-control finding asks "who may call this?", while every T9.016 case had the right caller and the wrong arithmetic or state transition. The 2026-10 split moved them here.

Recurring sub-shapes in the anchor set:

- **Missing solvency / health check on a value-moving path** — an action that should be refused when it leaves the actor undercollateralised is permitted on one path the designers did not guard (Euler 2023 `donateToReserves`; Platypus 2023 emergency withdrawal; Cypher 2023 aggregate sub-account debt; Rhea 2026 collateral taken from a user-supplied route claim).
- **State not cleared or updated after payout** — a record that should be consumed is left live and can be replayed (DoinGud 2026 `acceptBid`; Dream Health 2026 award reset; Likwid 2026 `leverage = 0` path skipping the reserve update).
- **Self-referential or aliasing accounting** — the same slot is read before and written after a mutation, or two claims are created on one asset (Miner ERC-X 2024 self-transfer cached balance; Yearn 2023 stale share-token mapping).
- **Invariant check computed wrong** — the constant-product or equivalent check exists but is miscalculated, overflows, or reads balances after an internal hook has moved the protocol's own money in (Uranium 2021; KyberSwap 2023 tick boundary; EtherVista 2026 uint112 product; MALT 2026 treasury DAI counted as trader input; GoldPesa 2026 unsettled hook deltas).
- **Edge-case branches in share math** — zero liquidity, zero supply, or first-depositor branches that stop being pro-rata (BitBay StableVault 2026).
- **Mispriced internal valuation** — the contract's own pricing function values an attacker-shaped input above its backing (Lien Finance 2026).
- **Distribution / reward accounting errors** — rewards accrue or distribute against the wrong basis (Compound 2021 Comptroller).

## Observed indicators

- A single transaction (often flash-loan funded) whose net effect is that the caller's withdrawable claim on the protocol rises by more than the value they supplied, through only public entrypoints.
- The same function called repeatedly in one transaction with the protocol's internal accounting not changing between calls (replayed claims, stale reserves).
- Calls on rarely-used branches: emergency withdrawal, zero-leverage paths, migration helpers, first-deposit or empty-pool states, self-transfers.
- Post-event: the root-cause analysis names an invariant ("total claims ≤ total assets", "debt ≤ collateral", "k never decreases") rather than a missing role.

## Detection signals

- **Pre-deployment invariant testing / formal verification (OAK-M16):** state the conservation invariants explicitly (assets vs. liabilities, k monotonicity, claim consumption) and fuzz every public entrypoint against them, including degenerate states (zero supply, self-transfer, `from == to`, zero leverage).
- **Per-transaction balance-sheet monitoring:** compare protocol assets and liabilities before and after each transaction; alert when a caller's net claim grows faster than their deposit, or when total liabilities exceed assets after a public call.
- **Repeated-call heuristics:** the same payout entrypoint invoked more than once per transaction by the same caller against the same record.
- **Rate limits / per-block caps (OAK-M11):** do not detect the bug but bound how much one transaction can extract against it.

## Reference implementations

- `mg-detectors-rs` — coverage **gap**. Balance-sheet delta monitoring per transaction is the generic detector; protocol-specific invariants require per-protocol configuration.

## Mitigations

- **OAK-M16 (Pre-Deployment Audit and Formal Verification):** highest leverage. Every T9.016 anchor violates an invariant that could have been written down and fuzzed.
- **OAK-M11 (Rate-Limiting and Per-Block Caps):** bounds single-transaction extraction.
- **OAK-M34 (Pause-by-Default / Emergency Pause):** limits the second and later transactions once monitoring fires.

**Canonical mitigations:** OAK-M16, OAK-M11.

## Discussion

**Boundary with T9.004.** Ask whether the attacker needed to be someone they were not. If the exploit works only because the contract accepted a caller, signer, account, module or counterparty it should have rejected — including a counterfeit object presented as genuine (Cashio's fake LP account, Crema's fake tick array, Exactly's fake market, FlashLoopAdapter's fake Safe) — it is T9.004. If any caller could have done it through public entrypoints with honest inputs and the contract simply computed the wrong result, it is T9.016. Cases with both (an unrestricted registration function *and* double-counted shares, as in Redsonic 2026-09) take T9.004 as primary when the missing gate was load-bearing, and cross-reference T9.016.

**Boundary with T9.011.** Rounding direction under integer division stays in T9.011. Wrong constants, overflow, missing updates and wrong branches are T9.016, even when they are arithmetic.

**Maturity.** Minted as `emerging` with seventeen re-homed anchors spanning 2021–2026; it inherits an established class rather than introducing a new one, so promotion is a question of the multi-source attestation bar, not of anchor count.
