# OAK-T9.016 — Contract Accounting / State-Invariant Flaw

**Parent Tactics:** OAK-T9 (Smart-Contract Exploit)
**Adjacent Techniques:** OAK-T9.004 (Access-Control Misconfiguration — the sibling: there, someone acted who should not have been allowed to act; here, the caller was entitled to the call and the contract's bookkeeping was wrong), OAK-T9.011 (Precision-Loss / Rounding Attack — the arithmetic sub-case where the error is rounding direction under integer division), OAK-T9.005 (Reentrancy — state read mid-update by a re-entering caller), OAK-T9.001 (Oracle Price Manipulation — the attacker moved an external price; here the contract mis-states its own internal value)
**Maturity:** emerging
**Chains:** chain-agnostic (EVM, Solana, Move-family, Starknet — the defect is in contract logic, not the runtime)
**First documented:** 2020-era DeFi at the class level; earliest corpus anchors Uranium Finance (2021-04, swap-invariant constant error) and Compound (2021-09, Comptroller distribution bug)
**Aliases:** "logic flaw", "accounting bug", "state-machine bug", "invariant violation", "missing solvency check", "contract-correctness bug", "business-logic exploit"

## Description

The contract lets a legitimate caller perform a legitimate call, and the call leaves the contract's books wrong in the caller's favour. No role is bypassed and no check of *who* is asking fails. What fails is the contract's model of *how much* — a balance, a reserve, a share price, a debt position, a claim record, a state flag — so that the attacker ends the transaction owed, or holding, more than they put in.

T9.016 exists because OAK previously filed these cases under T9.004 (Access-Control Misconfiguration), which described itself as "the residual category" for logic flaws. That grouping made the corpus's most-cited Technique mean two different things and pointed defenders at the wrong control: an access-control finding asks "who may call this?", while every T9.016 case had the right caller and the wrong arithmetic or state transition. The 2026-10 split moved them here.

Recurring sub-shapes in the anchor set:

- **Missing solvency / health check on a value-moving path** — an action that should be refused when it leaves the actor undercollateralised is permitted on one path the designers did not guard (Euler 2023 `donateToReserves`; Platypus 2023 emergency withdrawal; Cypher 2023 aggregate sub-account debt; Rhea 2026 collateral taken from a user-supplied route claim).
- **State not cleared or updated after payout** — a record that should be consumed is left live and can be replayed (DoinGud 2026 `acceptBid`; Dream Health 2026 award reset; Likwid 2026 `leverage = 0` path skipping the reserve update).
- **Self-referential or aliasing accounting** — the same slot is read before and written after a mutation, or two claims are created on one asset (Miner ERC-X 2024 self-transfer cached balance; Yearn 2023 stale share-token mapping).
- **Invariant check computed wrong** — the constant-product or equivalent check exists but is miscalculated, overflows, or reads balances after an internal hook has moved the protocol's own money in (Uranium 2021; KyberSwap 2023 tick boundary; EtherVista 2026 uint112 product; MALT 2026 treasury DAI counted as trader input; GoldPesa 2026 unsettled hook deltas).
- **Edge-case branches in share math** — zero liquidity, zero supply, or first-depositor branches that stop being pro-rata (BitBay StableVault 2026).
- **Mispriced internal valuation** — the contract's own pricing function values an attacker-shaped input above its backing (Lien Finance 2026).
- **Distribution / reward accounting errors** — rewards accrue or distribute against the wrong basis (Compound 2021 Comptroller).

## Observed indicators

- A single transaction (often flash-loan funded) whose net effect is that the caller's withdrawable claim on the protocol rises by more than the value they supplied, through only public entrypoints.
- The same function called repeatedly in one transaction with the protocol's internal accounting not changing between calls (replayed claims, stale reserves).
- Calls on rarely-used branches: emergency withdrawal, zero-leverage paths, migration helpers, first-deposit or empty-pool states, self-transfers.
- Post-event: the root-cause analysis names an invariant ("total claims ≤ total assets", "debt ≤ collateral", "k never decreases") rather than a missing role.

## Detection signals

- **Pre-deployment invariant testing / formal verification (OAK-M16):** state the conservation invariants explicitly (assets vs. liabilities, k monotonicity, claim consumption) and fuzz every public entrypoint against them, including degenerate states (zero supply, self-transfer, `from == to`, zero leverage).
- **Per-transaction balance-sheet monitoring:** compare protocol assets and liabilities before and after each transaction; alert when a caller's net claim grows faster than their deposit, or when total liabilities exceed assets after a public call.
- **Repeated-call heuristics:** the same payout entrypoint invoked more than once per transaction by the same caller against the same record.
- **Rate limits / per-block caps (OAK-M11):** do not detect the bug but bound how much one transaction can extract against it.

## Reference implementations

- `mg-detectors-rs` — coverage **gap**. Balance-sheet delta monitoring per transaction is the generic detector; protocol-specific invariants require per-protocol configuration.

## Mitigations

- **OAK-M16 (Pre-Deployment Audit and Formal Verification):** highest leverage. Every T9.016 anchor violates an invariant that could have been written down and fuzzed.
- **OAK-M11 (Rate-Limiting and Per-Block Caps):** bounds single-transaction extraction.
- **OAK-M34 (Pause-by-Default / Emergency Pause):** limits the second and later transactions once monitoring fires.
- **Check input shape and value all holdings at the vault entry point.** Enforced in `depositMulti`/`withdrawMulti`: require `amounts.length == tokens.length`, price the vault in every token it holds, and cap shares minted per deposit against deposit value. At ACryptoS (2026-11-08) a shortened amounts array dropped token1, which held most of the value, from the valuation, so a token0-only deposit minted 66.84% of a vault's shares; any one of the checks would have reverted the first transaction, before 135 addresses copied it. ([example](../examples/2026-11-acryptos-depositmulti-short-amounts-array-vault-share-mispricing.md))
- **Never move a pair's reserves from inside a token's transfer hook.** Enforced in the token and its tax-handling contract: do not let `PoolControl.sellToken` call a sender-restricted `controlburn` on the pair or call `pair.sync()` before the seller's transfer has arrived; swap tax tokens through a separate path after the trade settles. At TENDRIL (2026-11-04) each taxed sell burned the pair's own tokens and synced reserves first, so the delayed input withdrew almost all USDT from the pair; removing the burn-and-sync (or an invariant test that pair balances equal reserves after every sell) would have stopped the drain at that step. Contract source was not reviewed by OAK. ([example](../examples/2026-11-tendril-sell-hook-controlburn-pair-reserve-desync-pancakeswap-v2.md))
- **Post-liquidation solvency check and self-liquidation invariant test.** Enforced inside the lending contract's liquidation function, not in a front end or keeper: after every liquidation, revert if the borrower's remaining debt is non-zero while their collateral is zero, or if the market's withdrawable supply exceeds assets; fuzz the liquidation path with borrower equal to liquidator inside one flash-loan transaction. At MORE Markets (2026-11-16) liquidation cleared the attacker's collateral but not the matching WFLOW debt, so the check would have reverted at the step before WFLOW was withdrawn. The exact flawed branch is undisclosed. ([example](../examples/2026-11-more-markets-flow-evm-self-liquidation-cleared-collateral-not-wflow-debt.md))
- **Bound the interest index for every positive denominator, not just zero.** Enforced in the lending module's accrual code: require net supply to stay above a minimum floor (or reject any borrow that would push it into dust) and cap how far the supply index may move per block. At HARD Lend (2026-11-14) the division by net supply was skipped only at exactly zero, so a small BUSD loan left a dust supply and inflated deposit values ~243,000x; a floor or per-block index cap breaks that first step before any KAVA could be borrowed. See [HARD Lend](../examples/2026-11-hard-lend-kava-dormant-busd-market-supply-index-inflation-drain.md).
- **On-chain bounds on what a vault will buy or accept.** Enforced inside the vault program, not in the API that builds transactions: reject a loan whose rate exceeds a configured maximum, reject collateral whose pricing identifier does not match the deposited mint, and cap single-transaction NAV change and redemption size. At Loopscale (2026-11-05) the attacker assembled parameters off-chain and the program accepted them; a rate ceiling stops the NAV inflation at the step the loan is sold into the vault, an identity check stops kySOL being priced as zBTC, and a redemption cap bounds the attempted \$521K redemption. ([example](../examples/2026-11-loopscale-genesis-vaults-mispriced-collateral-and-nav-inflation-api-campaign.md))

- **Bound strategy parameters that reshape quotes, and keep a chain-level halt.** Enforced in the CosmWasm contract (cap how far one trade sequence can move a position's skew from its target; reject trades that push a position past the bound) and by validators' ability to halt the app layer. At Rujira (2026-11-10) the skew calculation let an attacker imbalance BTC/USDC CCL positions; a bound stops the first imbalancing trade, and the halt limited later ones. See [Rujira](../examples/2026-11-rujira-ccl-skew-calculation-flaw-btc-usdc-positions-imbalanced.md).
- **Restrict who can write a position record, and bound withdrawals by funded deposits.** Enforced inside each pool's snapshot and withdraw contracts: only the pool's own deposit and withdraw paths may update a user's recorded position, and every withdrawal reverts if it exceeds what that user's deposits funded. At Pool Party (2026-11-13) a recorded position was altered, a 10 USDC deposit made, and the pool's funds withdrawn, across 15 pools; either check breaks the first pool at the withdraw step. The flawed function is undisclosed. ([example](../examples/2026-11-pool-party-snapshot-position-record-manipulation-15-pools-arbitrum-base.md))
- **Settle reward checkpoints on every share transfer, and fuzz transfer-then-claim sequences.** Enforced inside the staking contract's transfer hook (settle sender and recipient before balances move) and in its pre-deployment invariant tests (total claimable rewards never exceed rewards funded, including to fresh recipient contracts). At Chain Games (2026-11-15) transfers left the recipient's checkpoint behind, so each new recipient could re-claim historical rewards through `restake()` across 360 cycles; a settle-on-transfer rule breaks the exploit at that step. Contract source was not reviewed by OAK. ([example](../examples/2026-11-chain-games-delegation-staking-share-transfer-reward-checkpoint-desync-polygon.md))

- **Bound payouts independent of the ledger, and reconcile subaccount transfers.** Enforced in the bridge contract (a per-window withdrawal cap the exchange backend cannot raise) and in the ledger's transfer path (a conservation check that the sum of a user's subaccount balances is unchanged by a transfer between them). At Arcus (2026-11-09) the bridge's on-chain rate limit held back \$3M of further requests and capped the payout at 2,234,391 USDG; a conservation check would have stopped the account recording a balance above what it held, at the first inflated transfer. The exact flawed condition is undisclosed. ([example](../examples/2026-11-arcus-subaccount-transfer-accounting-bug-inflated-balance-usdg-withdrawal.md))
- **Measure what a swap spent as well as what it returned.** Enforced in the vault: read its own balances of the input and output tokens before and after any router call and revert when the input spent differs from the amount the valuation used. At K3 Capital's sBOLD vault (2026-11-18) only the returned BOLD was checked, so WETH left in the vault was counted when shares were redeemed ([`examples/2026-11-k3-capital-sbold-vault-swap-spent-token-unchecked-share-redemption-ethereum.md`](../examples/2026-11-k3-capital-sbold-vault-swap-spent-token-unchecked-share-redemption-ethereum.md)).

**Canonical mitigations:** OAK-M16, OAK-M11.

## Discussion

**Boundary with T9.004.** Ask whether the attacker needed to be someone they were not. If the exploit works only because the contract accepted a caller, signer, account, module or counterparty it should have rejected — including a counterfeit object presented as genuine (Cashio's fake LP account, Crema's fake tick array, Exactly's fake market, FlashLoopAdapter's fake Safe) — it is T9.004. If any caller could have done it through public entrypoints with honest inputs and the contract simply computed the wrong result, it is T9.016. Cases with both (an unrestricted registration function *and* double-counted shares, as in Redsonic 2026-09) take T9.004 as primary when the missing gate was load-bearing, and cross-reference T9.016.

**Boundary with T9.011.** Rounding direction under integer division stays in T9.011. Wrong constants, overflow, missing updates and wrong branches are T9.016, even when they are arithmetic.

**Maturity.** Minted as `emerging` with seventeen re-homed anchors spanning 2021–2026; it inherits an established class rather than introducing a new one, so promotion is a question of the multi-source attestation bar, not of anchor count.
