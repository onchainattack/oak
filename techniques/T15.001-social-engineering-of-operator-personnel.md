# OAK-T15.001 — Social Engineering of Operator Personnel

**Parent Tactics:** OAK-T15 (Off-chain Entry-Vector / Pre-Positioning)
**Maturity:** emerging
**Chains:** chain-agnostic (the social-engineering vector is off-chain; downstream on-chain manifestation is per-incident)
**First documented:** systematically 2019 onward (DragonEx WFC Proof "trading-bot" lure as the canonical pre-2020 anchor); the LinkedIn fake-job-offer / Telegram fake-recruiter / fake-investor / fake-trading-bot variants stabilised across 2020–2026 OAK-G01 operations
**Aliases:** "TraderTraitor entry vector", "LinkedIn fake-job-offer", "fake-recruiter lure", "DPRK fake-coding-test", "fake-investor pretext", "Penpie audit-report lure", "WFC Proof", "Contagious Interview / Wagemole"

## Description

Targeted social engineering directed at operator-side personnel — developers, signers, executives, security-team staff, customer-support staff with privileged access — at exchanges, custody vendors, signing-platform vendors, bridges, DeFi protocols, and trader-tooling shops. The defining shape is **pretext + payload**: the attacker establishes a credible operator-relevant pretext (recruiter approaching a developer with a senior role; VC/investor reaching out to a founder; trading-firm-or-API partner approaching an exchange engineer; former-contractor reaching out via Telegram with an "audit report" attachment), and the pretext delivers a payload (a "coding test" repository whose dependencies trigger a backdoor; a malware-laden PDF / ZIP / DMG; a fake trading-bot or analytics application; a Calendly-class flow that drops a bin file; an `npm install` whose lifecycle hook runs a backdoor — note that `prepare` fires on a bare `npm install`, so the trigger is not confined to `postinstall`, and a loader checked into application source rather than a dependency survives `--ignore-scripts` entirely).

T15.001 is the **vector**, not the resulting endpoint state. The endpoint state — implant established, MITM layer planted, signing-host RPC compromised, browser extension exfiltrating cookies — is OAK-T15.003 (Operator-Endpoint Compromise). The two are typically chained: a successful T15.001 produces a T15.003 endpoint state, which then enables a downstream T11.x / T9.x / T10.x event. Splitting them lets defenders independently prioritise anti-phishing controls (T15.001 mitigation: OAK-M21) and signing-host hardening (T15.003 mitigation: OAK-M19, EDR / MDM).

T15.001 is **structurally distinct** from end-user phishing (T4-class). End-user phishing's victim is the wallet-holder; T15.001's victim is an operator-side staff member whose access controls a downstream signing surface that many third-party customers depend on. The dollar-loss-prevented metric is therefore not per-victim but per-operator-customer-base: a single successful T15.001 against a Safe{Wallet} developer (Bybit Feb 2025) or a Ginco engineer (DMM May 2024) produced losses that distributed across the vendor's entire customer base.

The dominant pretext channels at v0.1 are LinkedIn (the foundational TraderTraitor / Lazarus channel from ~2019 onward), Telegram (the Radiant Capital 2024 variant — impersonation of a former contractor delivering an "audit report"), Discord / Slack DMs (fake-investor / fake-VC pretexts; fake-bug-bounty submissions), and email (recruiter pretexts plus fake-investor outreach). The channel rotates per operator's social posture; the social-engineering shape — credible operator-relevant pretext + payload that compromises the workstation — is constant.

## Observed indicators

- **Off-chain (operator-side):** unsolicited recruiter / investor / partner messages targeting personnel with privileged access (multisig signers, deployer-key holders, signing-platform engineers, customer-support staff with UI privilege); attachments or repositories whose execution requires running untrusted code (npm install, Python installation, DMG / ZIP execution, PDF with unusual permissions); credibility-laundering patterns where the pretext references a real prior project / employer / product to lower scrutiny.
- **Pretext sub-shapes:**
  - *Fake-job-offer / fake-recruiter* — most common DPRK pattern; payload is typically a "coding test" or interview repository; canonical anchors at Ronin (LinkedIn), DMM Bitcoin (LinkedIn coding test), Drift Protocol (LinkedIn six-month rapport-building); also DragonEx 2019 WFC Proof trading-bot lure as pre-2020 anchor. A 2026 variant ships the lure repository with **no payload in it at all** — only a pointer to a mutable record on an anonymous public JSON-hosting service, fetched at import time and executed via `Function.constructor`, which makes repository file hashes uninformative about what will actually run and lets the operator change the payload after delivery (OnChainAttack maintainer, 2026-08).
  - *Fake-investor / fake-VC* — payload is typically a "deck" PDF or a Calendly-class flow that drops a bin; canonical anchor at Concentric Finance (fabricated-investor pretext to one of the multisig signers).
  - *Fake-trading-bot / fake-API-partner* — payload is a DMG / installer claiming to provide trading or analytics tooling; canonical anchor at DragonEx (2019 WFC Proof) and the broader 2024-2026 SwiftLoader / RustBucket macOS-targeted family.
  - *Fake-former-contractor / fake-audit-report* — payload is a ZIP claiming to contain a security report or post-mortem; canonical anchor at Radiant Capital (Telegram impersonation, `Penpie_Hacking_Analysis_Report.zip`).
  - *Fake-staking-partner / fake-third-party-API* — payload exploits the trust relationship between an operator and a known partner; canonical anchor at SwissBorg (Kiln staking-partner third-party API as the trust-substrate).
- **Forensic record:** mailbox / DM archive of the pretext, attachment file hashes, post-event endpoint forensics establishing the payload-to-implant chain.

## Detection signals

Signals are ordered cheapest-first, and every one of them is available **before a payload is offered**. That ordering is deliberate: the 2026-08 anchor's delivered repository contained no attacker code at all, so a defensive posture that waits for something detectable to arrive has already conceded. The identity layer is where this class is cheap to catch.

- **Age of the identity, measured against the date of first contact.** Check the sender domain's registration date (`whois`, creation date) and the delivering platform account's creation date. In the 2026-08 anchor the recruiter domain was **78 days old** and the GitHub account that delivered the repository was **9 days old**, its profile completed 70 seconds after creation and never touched again, with zero public repositories and zero followers. A firm recruiting senior engineers has a domain older than its hiring cycle; a project manager who has held a job has an account older than the week they contacted you.
- **Claimed affiliation verified out-of-band, against a channel the claimed employer controls.** The persona in the 2026-08 anchor claimed employment at a major Ethereum software firm — a one-minute check against that company's own directory. Note that the impersonated organisation need not be the sender's domain: that campaign borrowed **two different real companies** at two different stages, a genomics firm's name for the recruiter domain and an Ethereum software firm for the GitHub persona.
- **Domain-twin check.** Established companies do not usually run hiring from a freshly-registered TLD variant of a name they already hold. `dantelabs.io` (2026) against `dantelabs.com` (2015, a real and unrelated company) is the shape.
- **Process compression as a behavioural signal.** A hiring process described in five steps from which the first human-contact step is then removed on CV strength is optimising for time-to-execution. It is observable before any code changes hands and requires no technical analysis.
- **Mail-authentication results read for what they actually assert.** SPF pass proves the sender controls the domain — which an attacker who registered it does. In the 2026-08 anchor SPF passed through a mainstream commercial provider while DKIM returned `permerror` (no key for signature), and the GitHub invitation passed SPF, DKIM and DMARC because it was genuine platform mail about hostile content. Authentication answers *did this come from that domain*, never *is that domain trustworthy*.
- **Mail hygiene of the claimed employer's domain, as a cheap corroborating check.** Two DNS lookups: does the domain publish a DKIM key for the selector its mail is signed with, and does it publish a DMARC policy at all? The 2026-08 anchor's domain signed against a selector with **no published key** and had **no DMARC record**, a state that persists after the campaign. An organisation large enough to run structured senior-engineer hiring generally has both working; a domain registered for one campaign generally has neither. Weak on its own, strong in combination with domain age.
- **Operator-side mailbox / DM monitoring** for unsolicited contacts directed at privileged staff; flag any pretext whose next step requires executing code in order to evaluate it.
- **Anti-phishing simulation** targeted at recruiter / investor / partner pretexts (not just generic email phishing) — rehearse the "LinkedIn cold-message → coding-test repository" pattern, the "Telegram former-contractor → audit-report ZIP" pattern, and the "fake-investor → deck-PDF / Calendly" pattern.
- **Manifest review before any package-manager command**, and the handling invariant that follows from it — see [`OAK-M48`](../mitigations/OAK-M48-unsolicited-code-quarantine.md).
- **CTI feed integration** — subscribe to vendor-side advisories on active recruiter-pretext campaigns (Mandiant UNC4736 / Citrine Sleet / TraderTraitor reporting; Unit 42 BeaverTail / Contagious Interview; Microsoft Sapphire Sleet / Storm-1811 reporting); cross-reference against operator-personnel inbox traffic.

## Mitigations

- **OAK-M21 (Anti-Phishing Training for Privileged Staff):** highest-leverage layer — rehearse the recruiter / investor / former-contractor / partner-API pretexts specifically; treat any unsolicited code from a recruiter / investor / partner channel as actively hostile until proven otherwise.
- **OAK-M48 (Unsolicited-Code Quarantine and Pre-Execution Manifest Review):** the layer that makes pretext success survivable — unsolicited code is evaluated on a machine that holds nothing, or not at all, and every manifest in the tree is read as text before any package-manager command. M48 exists because the three checks developers actually perform — read the code, install with `--ignore-scripts`, install but do not run — **all pass** against the 2026-08 anchor's construction. Invest here rather than in pretext recognition: that pretext ran to completion against the maintainer of this page.
- **OAK-M22 (Rotate-on-Disclosure Discipline):** when an operator-side staff member discloses a suspected pretext interaction, treat the staff member's local credential set, signing material, and active sessions as compromised and rotate.
- **Operator-side OSINT:** monitor public references to your engineers / signers in adversary-controlled channels; treat targeted social-media outreach to identified privileged staff as elevated risk.
- **Cohort-level CTI:** maintain subscription to vendor advisories on active recruiter-pretext campaigns; cross-reference against operator-personnel inbox traffic.

## Discussion

T15.001's structural lesson is that operator-side personnel are an attack surface in their own right, not merely an extension of the vendor perimeter. The 2024–2026 OAK-G01 wave (DMM, WazirX, Radiant, Bybit, Drift Protocol) made this legible at scale: four out of the five canonical incidents in that wave landed via T15.001, the channel rotated (LinkedIn → Telegram → fake-API-partner) but the social-engineering shape was constant, and the dollar-aggregate exceeded \$2B. For OAK's purposes, T15.001 is the **highest-leverage mitigation point** in the supply-chain attack class: every successful pretext-recognition event prevents an entire downstream T11 / T9 / T10 chain.

The DPRK-attribution share is high but not exclusive. The pretext-and-payload pattern generalises: any sufficiently motivated adversary can rent the kit (BeaverTail / InvisibleFerret are open-source-adjacent at this point), and OAK contributors writing future T15.001 examples should not assume DPRK attribution by default. Attribution belongs at the OAK-Gnn layer based on the cluster-forensic record, not at the Technique layer.

False-positive considerations: legitimate recruiter / investor / partner outreach is genuinely common in operator-side workflows. The detection signal is not "the pretext exists" but "the pretext requires executing unsolicited code." Operator-side training should focus on recognising the **payload-vector** step, not on rejecting all unsolicited contact.

v0.x deferred items: a clean boundary between T15.001 and adversarial-insider patterns (a hostile insider whose access was originally legitimate is structurally distinct from a pretext-induced grant of access; OAK does not yet have a Technique covering insider-threat surfaces and may need a separate sub-Technique once the public record matures); cohort-level metrics on time-from-first-contact-to-payload-execution and time-from-payload-to-on-chain-extraction (Drift Protocol's six-month rapport-building was unusually long; the median is under 30 days, and the OnChainAttack 2026-08 controlled engagement ran cold-email-to-code-on-disk in about **five hours** against a target who introduced no friction at any step, which is close to a clean upper-bound measurement of *operator tempo* rather than of victim susceptibility — a figure the cohort median cannot supply, because unwitting victims always add delay).
