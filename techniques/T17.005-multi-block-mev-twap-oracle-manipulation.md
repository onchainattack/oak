# OAK-T17.005 — TWAP Oracle Manipulation via Multi-Block MEV (DEPRECATED)

**Maturity:** deprecated
**Superseded by:** none — the mechanism is returned to [`TAXONOMY-GAPS.md`](../TAXONOMY-GAPS.md) as a candidate; single-block and funded price manipulation of an oracle is [OAK-T9.001](./T9.001-oracle-price-manipulation.md)
**Deprecated:** 2026-11-27

## Why this ID is deprecated

The Technique described an attacker who controls **two or more consecutive blocks** (as a proposer, or by colluding with one) and uses them to move a time-weighted average price oracle. The 2026-11-27 source pass found the mechanism in a research paper, Mackinga, Nadahalli and Wattenhofer, "TWAP Oracle Attacks: Easier Done than Said?" ([IACR ePrint 2022/445](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/445)), and found no incident.

- Its two worked examples were cohort files with no named, sourced member. Both were quarantined on 2026-11-27 (see `CORRECTIONS.md`).
- The one real incident they mentioned, Inverse Finance (2022-04-02), is described by RedStone as a large swap sent in a bundle directly to miners, with the oracle keeping the manipulated price in the next block. RedStone does not report proposer collusion or control of consecutive blocks. It is filed under OAK-T9.001: see [`examples/2022-04-inverse-finance-twap.md`](../examples/2022-04-inverse-finance-twap.md).
- The page was rated `observed` and cited "Flashbots research" and builder-concentration figures that no opened page carries.

A mechanism shown only in theory does not meet OAK's bar of a documented incident. The ID is retained and not reused, per `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## What is real nearby

- **Oracle price manipulation within one transaction or one block**, with or without a flash loan, is OAK-T9.001.
- **Manipulating a time window that a treasury or vesting contract settles on** is OAK-T17.004.
- The defensive point of the paper stands on its own and belongs to OAK-T9.001's mitigations: a longer TWAP window raises the cost of manipulation, and a protocol should not assume that an attacker cannot hold a price across a block boundary.

If an incident is documented in which control of consecutive blocks was used to move an oracle, it goes to TAXONOMY-GAPS as a candidate and is minted under a new ID.

## What went wrong, for the record

The page and its two cohorts were written from the paper's idea and from general knowledge of proposer-builder separation, with loss ranges ("mid-seven-figures"), block counts and builder-concentration figures added without a source. Same failure as OAK-T9.012, OAK-T10.008 and OAK-T16.003.
