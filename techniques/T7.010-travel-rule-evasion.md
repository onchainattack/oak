# OAK-T7.010 — Travel Rule Evasion (DEPRECATED)

**Maturity:** deprecated
**Superseded by:** none — the laundering legs it was attached to are [OAK-T7.003](./T7.003-cross-chain-bridge-laundering.md) and [OAK-T7.005](./T7.005-privacy-chain-hops.md)
**Deprecated:** 2026-11-27

## Why this ID is deprecated

The Technique described launderers who **structure transfers below a Travel Rule threshold, or route through non-compliant providers or jurisdictions, in order to avoid Travel Rule data sharing**. The 2026-11-27 source passes found no documented case.

- Its policy anchor was a file whose only opened source was a law-firm summary of a FATF update on how far countries had implemented the rule. It describes no attacker and was quarantined.
- **The 4,064 BTC theft of 2024-08-19.** No opened page mentions the Travel Rule, a threshold or data sharing; exchanges appear only as destinations in the investigator's alert. The file now declares the bridge and privacy-conversion Techniques only.
- **The 2026-07 report on North Korean operators.** Its two sources relay one anonymous account of transfers split into small amounts at informal brokers in China. That is structuring of cash-outs, with no provider, threshold or Travel Rule in the sources.
- A search of government actions and analytics-firm case studies found no laundering case described in Travel Rule terms.

With no documented incident behind it, the ID does not meet OAK's bar. The ID is retained and not reused, per `CONTRIBUTING.md`.

## What is real nearby

- **Structuring below a reporting threshold** is real as a behaviour. The one documented lead is regulatory: crypto.news reports that South Korea removed its 1 million won Travel Rule threshold in 2026-08 after a case of about 200 million won of USDT withdrawn in 216 transfers, each under the threshold. The regulator's own announcement was not opened, and the case is not shown to involve stolen funds. It is recorded in TAXONOMY-GAPS as a candidate.
- **Cross-chain hops and conversion to privacy assets**, which do appear in the incidents this ID was attached to, are OAK-T7.003 and OAK-T7.005.

## What went wrong, for the record

The page turned a compliance concept into an attacker Technique and attached it to laundering cases whose sources never mention the rule. Percentages of "non-compliant" volume and jurisdiction lists were written without a source. Same failure as OAK-T9.012, OAK-T10.008, OAK-T16.003 and OAK-T17.005.
